## Full English translation

### Printed page 147

#### Ome

Ome is number two.

Number two in Maya is ka.

Quetzalhuitzitzilli, companion of number two.

In nature it is the simplest manifestation of duality, which is found in everything symmetrical and even in everything that, although asymmetrical, has two complementary parts. Symmetry in nature amazes us with its perfection when we contemplate a butterfly's wings, the leaves of plants, or the feathers of precious birds.

The human body is not exempt from this symmetry, since most of its principal organs are dual, such as the lungs, the cerebral hemispheres, the testicles in men, and the ovaries in women. The arms with their hands and the legs with their feet have the same condition of symmetry and duality. Other organs that are single, such as the heart, also have duality in their principal parts. By generating duality we understand the essence that forms, according to traditional teachings, everything our eyes contemplate. That is literally what they tell us in general terms, before explaining that everything our eyes see, the stones, clouds, people, birds, trees, and plants, is made up of male parts and female parts. This simple explanation has also helped us better understand the comments of Fray Pedro de los Ríos, in which he mentions that in Omeyohcan, the place of dual essence, dwelt the Lord and Lady of Duality called Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl. On joining through the attractive force of Omeyotl, they form Ometeotl. Fray Pedro de los Ríos calls him Hometeule, attributing to him the qualities of the Christians' God the Father. In Indigenous thought, Omeyohcan is the place containing the essence of duality, Omeyotl, which is the force of attraction that joins particles and beings of opposite polarities, forming units that are plural in their integral essence.

Xochiquetzalli butterfly.

This union is the product of the action of Ometeotl as the generating principle, and everything emanating from him is the product of the union of masculine and feminine particles, resulting in elements and beings that are all dual in nature in their primordial essence.

Ome as a noun derives from omitl, bones. With them, the shape of the human body and of all vertebrate animals is structured.

The symbolism of number two when applied to people is union, complement, formation, and ambivalence. To this we may add structure and inner solidity, because great value is placed on firm Indigenous values grounded in respect and in the principle or esteem of duality.

### Printed page 148

The bird companion of number two is the quetzalhuitzizilli, the beautiful hummingbird with jade-green plumage, associated with activity but above all with the restlessness of seeking, as a couple, complete freedom to raise the spirit toward less worldly and ordinary levels. These characteristics resemble those of the quetzaltototl, the beautiful bird of our country's southeast, with feathers the translucent green color of jade. Balancing this tendency toward spirituality, the companion of number two among the thirteen daytime lords is Tlaltecuhtli, who represents the masculine part of the Earth. In him there is sound judgment and firmness of character.

In the Tonalamatl of Aubin, Ixtlilton emerges from the beak of the Quetzalhuitzizilli, the one with the black face. This figure is said to be a quality we all have in youth, with which we can feel and understand, and even provide relief to whoever needs it. In the historical sources they translate Ixtlilton as the little black one.

Ixtlilton emerging from the beak of the Quetzalhuitzizilli. Tonalamatl of Aubin.

The engineer David Esparza Hidalgo, author of *Aztec Computation*, used to tell us, illustrating the meaning of duality in everyday life, that a person has freedom of movement in any direction, like a marble that can roll freely. But when two beings complement each other adequately in their relationship, it is as though two marbles were stuck together and could only roll together, always in the same direction.

Summarizing all the data concerning number two, to be applied to people related to its characteristics, we arrange them similarly to the list for the preceding number.

Ome, two. Complementarity, duality, and ambivalence.

Quetzalhuitzitzilli. Beautiful hummingbird. Very active people with a beautiful presence who inwardly always seek to raise their spirituality through the application of their inner values.

Tlaltecuhtli with the beautiful hummingbird. Borbonicus Codex.

Tlaltecuhtli. The lord of the Earth. It is the link between human beings and everything on the Earth's surface, in tlalticpac. It represents firm character and sound judgments directed toward community work.

Ixtlilton. Youthful spirit, sensitivity to know what is happening to the people with whom one lives, willingness to support whoever needs help.
