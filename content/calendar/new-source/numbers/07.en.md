## Full English translation

### Printed page 157

#### Chicome

Chicome is the number seven.

In the Maya language it is *uuk*.

In the Nahuatl language it is called *chicome* because it is the number two placed before and joined to the number five. Its hieroglyph is a hand with two dots or two fingers. In the system of dots and bars, it is represented by one bar and two dots.

In oral tradition the number seven is mentioned as the flight of thought that admits no limitations, like butterflies that move tirelessly from one place to another. At a particular moment in the lunar phases, thought becomes active, influencing intellect and intuition. This relationship is the reason why, in many ancient cultures, seven is taken as a lunar number, since one quarter of a cycle recorded from one new Moon to the next new Moon, passing through the first quarter, the full Moon, and the last quarter, is seven days and a small fraction.

The number seven, like five and six, belongs to the second stage of integration through learning, an important stage for inner transformation arising from the restlessness of our thoughts. Chicome regulates the action of Metztli Tezcatlipoca on our psychological development, and the life-generating action through the lunar influence of Tecuziztecatl Quetzalcoatl in the fertility cycles of the female beings of nature.

It is accompanied by Centeotl-Xochipilli, in these two aspects of solar energy used to produce vegetable sustenance through Centeotl, and for dedication to botany, both ornamental and the production of healing plants, through Xochipilli. In people it manifests, besides love for nature and plants, as great ease in expressing emotions and moods through art in any of its forms, since there is great creativity born of the sensitivity of the intellect.

The flying companion, on this one occasion in the series of thirteen companions of the numbers, is an ash-colored butterfly called *tizapapalotl*, placed at the center of the list in the *Borgia Codex* and the *Borbónico Codex*. In the *Tonalamatl de Aubin*, it is simply a yellow-orange butterfly, related to solar energy used by the intellect.

### Printed page 158

The butterfly as solar energy is also a symbol of another form of energy that always arrives perpendicularly at any point on Earth, as a precious energy represented by a stylized butterfly forming the pectoral of the Lord of Celestial Heat, who comes from above to penetrate the Earth, leaving his fertilizing charge.

The pectoral of a Xiuhtecuhtli has this form, and it is precisely Xiuhtecuhtli who emerges from the mouth of the *tizapapalotl* butterfly, giving human beings the quality of honesty and justice for centered beings.

*Xiuhtecuhtli. Tonalamatl de Aubin.*

**Chicome.** The relationship of the mind's activity with the influence of the Moon's phases.

**Xochipilli.** It is creativity through art and familiarity with the plant world.

**Tizapapalotl.** It is the butterfly, a symbol of solar energy and freedom of thought.

*Borgia Codex.*

**Xiuhtecuhtli.** The Lord of Centrality and Justice.
