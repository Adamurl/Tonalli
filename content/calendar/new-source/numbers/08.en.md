## Full English translation

### Printed page 158

#### Chicueyi

Chicueyi is the number eight.

In the Maya language it is called *uaxak* and is represented with one bar and three dots.

The Mexica hieroglyph is a hand and three fingers.

Chicueyi is the third finger of the hand that joins the other complete hand, as one more step toward bringing the two hands together.

### Printed page 159

In the number eight, the influence coming from the four directions of the world, bringing winds and rains, joins everything that reaches us from the world's four corners. The second stage of four numbers of constant learning is completed at this level. It is the experience of accumulated teachings integrated into physical development. The inner essence manifests by raising the spirit from the earliest conditions of a being that was formed organically as a suitable vessel in which a cosmic essence has been deposited, giving the great responsibility of being worthy bearers of it.

The number eight is accompanied by the Lord of Rain, the one who is the liquid the Earth drinks, called Tlaloc in the Mexican language and Chac in the Maya language.

Tlaloc represents the force of the hurricane and the storm, although he also manifests through gentle, caressing rains. He is the Lord of Clouds and Lightning, of unbreakable tenacity, and of the regenerating fertility of water.

The bird accompanying the number eight is the shining eagle, *itzcuauhtli* in the *Borgia Codex*, or simply *cuauhtli* in the *Borbónico Codex* and the *Tonalamatl de Aubin*, as a symbol of freedom and the high aspirations achieved with decisiveness and impulsiveness.

In the *Tonalamatl de Aubin*, Tlaloc's face emerges from the eagle's beak, reinforcing the influence of the Lord of Rain for people who carry his characteristics.

The summary is made up as follows:

**Chicueyi.** Integration through constant learning, gathering the experience of life from what surrounds us.

**Tlaloc.** Strength in decisions and gentleness in dealings. These are people with a changeable character that one must know how to keep at the center between extremes.

**Cuauhtli.** Splendor, decisiveness, impulsiveness, and high objectives. The eagle always seeks freedom of action, makes quick decisions, and hits the target precisely.

**Tlaloc.** Once again the Lord of Rain appears, reinforcing the qualities of the daytime companion.
