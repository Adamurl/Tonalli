## Full English translation

### Printed page 149

#### Yei-Eyi

Yei is number three. In the Maya language it is ox.

It is common to find numeral three associated with iconographic elements related to water. At Teotihuacan, paintings with female figures who pour out the precious liquid with outstretched hands, fertilizing the Earth, have the numeral on the mouth from which water flows, or on the headdress. It is also frequently found on the attire of Tlaloc, the Earth's liquid, who represents the water that comes down vertically from the clouds.

Teotihuacan representation of the lady of water with numeral three on her mouth. Tepantitla mural.

The water that runs in rivers and fills lakes and lagoons is represented by Chalchihuitl-icue, she whose skirt is adorned with jades. Rivers are the arteries, and water is the blood of Mother Earth.

Yei or eyi may derive from the Mexican-language name for the vital liquid that runs through the arteries of our organism, our blood, which in the Mexican language is called yeztli or eztli. Blood runs without obstacles through our body. It is a liquid that, the hotter it is, induces human beings to behave with more courage and daring, with a greater capacity for decision and more strength of will to overcome the obstacles that arise in everyday life. In this condition, the metaphor of burned water, atlachinolli, becomes evident in people's behavior.

Lady 7-Flint with the attributes of Chalchiuhtlicue at the edge of an ameyalli. Plate 22, Nuttall Codex.

Number three, associated with water and blood as vital liquids, represents fluidity, strength, and decision. It also has a meaning of the feminine and of balance. Pre-Hispanic vessels that have only three supports can sit perfectly on a surface that is not very level. Associating the preceding factors with human behavior, for there to be fluidity in a couple, the woman's intuition and order regulate the man's impulsiveness, producing balance. This feminine quality is represented by the three stones of the tlecuili, the hearth, and by the three supports of the metate. In some vessels, the three supports are female breasts, like the quality of balance that a woman possesses.

### Printed page 150

The relationship of number three with the element water is reaffirmed by the identity of its daytime companion, Chalchiuhtlicue, she whose skirt is adorned with jade. In colonial-period documents, she is mentioned as the feminine spirit of flowing waters who lives in rivers, ponds, lagoons, and at the seashore.

Now we need only incorporate into the characteristics the flying companion of number three, a bird that lives in the heights, is swift and splendid in bearing, although not large. It is called tohtli in the Mexican language, the falcon. In the tonalamatl it represents youth, impulsiveness, and daring, despite its size, which is much smaller than an eagle's.

Xochipilli, the child of the flowers, emerges from the falcon's beak. He represents the relationship some people have with botany, art, and color. In their main characteristics, they are people of beautiful appearance. They are also very creative, with great manual skill, and they like dance and music.

The summary is as follows.

Yei-eyi, three. Fluidity, intuition, decision, and balance. These are people for whom there are no obstacles that cannot be overcome. They never leave their course, and with strength of will and character they reach their proposed goals.

Chalchiuhtlicue as daytime companion. From the beak of Tohtli the falcon emerges Xochipilli, the child of the flowers. Tonalamatl of Aubin.

Chalchihuitl i cue, Chalchiuhtlicue. Their characteristics resemble those of the sign atl, water: transparency, adaptability, fluidity. They are very sensitive, but with great strength of will. They adapt to any situation, and in their way of being they always act transparently.

Tohtli. They like to fly very high, are agile in their movements, and pleasant in appearance. Despite their sensitivity, they are tenacious until they achieve their objectives.

Xochipilli. They are closely connected with plants. They have great respect and affection for nature, are creative, and have the ability to express themselves in several forms related to art, using movement and color.
