## Full English translation

### Printed page 179

#### Companions of the trecenas that form the Tonalpohualli

##### First trecena, 1-Cipactli

Regarding the rulers of this trecena, Francisco del Paso y Troncoso says the following in the *Historical Description and Exposition of the Borbonicus Codex*:

> First thirteen-day period. Sign Ce Cipaktli. Divine beings Tonakatéuktli and Tonakaciuatl.

The Vaticanus Latinus Codex 3738 reaffirms Troncoso's supposition, because the first two plates are missing from the Borbonicus Codex. On plates XV and XVI of Vaticanus Latinus, Tonacatecuhtli and Tonacacihuatl accompany the first trecena. On plate I of the second part of the Telleriano Remensis Codex, Tonacacihuatl appears as Chicomecoatl, and at the sides are the names Xochiquetzalli and Xumico, the second alluding to one of the oldest names of the Earth, Oxomoco. We know that Tonacacihuatl is the same Omecihuatl who, by forming a duality with Ometecuhtli, makes up the essence of Ometeotl from which all beings and all things are made. As feminine energy she manifests in Xochiquetzalli with all the beauty that exists in Oxomoco, the ancient name for Mother Earth. As Chicomecoatl she is in the sustenance that sprouts from the Earth. Undoubtedly, several functions of a feminine nature derive from Tonacacihuatl, forming a duality with Tonacatecuhtli, deposited in the first group of thirteen days.

In the Tonalamatl of Aubin and the Borbonicus, the first two plates are missing. The Borgia Codex is the only pre-Hispanic testimony we have. On plate 61 there is only Tonacatecuhtli as ruler of ce cipactli, the first trecena.

This trecena will serve as an example for all the others:

**Thirteen days, ce cipactli.** Distributed over the thirteen days, it represents the same qualities that the sign cipactli has as a day, which are:

**Firm principles. Initiative. Responsibility. Gift for command. Tenacity.**

**Companions, Tonacatecuhtli and Tonacacihuatl, for the thirteen days.**

The reeds, tona acatl, are arrows of heat that translate as firm goals and defined objectives. Once personified, tecuh and cihua, they represent an energetic, kind, centered character, with qualities of command.

#### Second trecena, Ce Ocelotl

All the sources in which the Tonalpohualli is mentioned agree that the companion of this trecena is Quetzalcoatl, whom we see on plate 62 of the Borgia Codex.

**Thirteen days, ce ocelotl.**

**Cautious. Calculating. Meticulous. Sure of themselves. Splendid bearing.**

**Companion, Quetzalcoatl.** Beautiful serpent made up of all the elements that produce life on Earth. It represents fertile creativity that leads to a search for spirituality. It is beautiful energy that becomes a spirit of service full of goodness and affection. It is a restless desire to seek what is truly transcendent.

#### Third trecena, Ce Mazatl

Rulers in the Borbonicus Codex: Tepeyolohtli and Tlazohteotl. In the Telleriano Remensis Codex and Vaticanus Latinus, Tepeyolohtli and Quetzalcoatl appear as companions.

**Thirteen, ce mazatl. Restless, sensitive, attentive to what happens; they like to be free.**

**Companion, Tepeyolohtli.** Heart of the Mountain means constant self-evaluation, introspection, control of emotions, and inner strength that one must know how to balance.

#### Fourth trecena, Ce Xochitl

In the Telleriano Codex, Huehuecoyotl is accompanied by Xochiquetzalli, in the Borgia and in Vaticanus B. She is the same Xochiquetzal whom the interpreter of the latter calls Ysnextli, and Eduard Seler, Ixnextli, she whose face is covered with ashes.

### Printed page 181

Ixnextli is one of the most beautiful feminine figures drawn in the pre-Hispanic amoxtin. In reality she is Xochiquetzalli representing the qualities of a young woman at puberty who does not want people to notice them.

In the Borbonicus Codex, accompanying the old coyote is a musician playing a huehuetl, with a design on his face similar to Xochipilli's.

The qualities are the following:

**Thirteen days, Ce Xochitl. Beauty and physical presence, creativity, agility, positive purposes that bring everything begun to completion.**

**Companions, Huehuecoyotl and Ixnextli.** Through Huehuecoyotl, there is agility, dance, exercise, rhythm, and musicality. In this companion is integrated the capacity, in mature age, to manifest sexuality fully through the strength acquired by practicing dance. Through Ixnextli, there are psychic qualities that have no relationship with the material world, since they manifest according to the moods that arise in each person.

Illustration caption: Ce Xochitl, Borbonicus Codex.

#### Fifth trecena, Ce Acatl

In the Telleriano Remensis Codex, Chalchiuhtlicue is accompanied by Tlazohteotl. In the Borgia and the Borbonicus, only Chalchiuhtlicue appears as ruler.

**Thirteen, Ce Acatl.**

**Intelligent, analytical, harsh in their judgments, centered, and not very emotional.**

**Companions, Chalchihuitl icue and Tlazohteotl.** She who has her skirt of jade represents the fluidity of water, tenacity and constancy, transparency, and the ability to adapt to circumstances. Tlazohteotl represents the heat of the Earth, the affection with which she provides everything for her children, which translates into a constant manifestation of love and protection toward those close to her.

#### Sixth trecena, Ce Miquiztli

In the Telleriano Codex, Tonatiuh is accompanied by Metztli, the Moon. In the Borbonicus Codex, Tonatiuh is accompanied by a youthful-looking Tezcatlipoca identified with Titlacahuan, whom we all possess. In the Borgia Codex, as we see in the illustration from plate 66, in his place is another figure called Tecziztecatl, who represents the Moon in another of its functions.

**Thirteen, ce miquiztli. Rest, constant renewal, transformation, reflection, and daring.**

**Companions, Tonatiuh and Tecziztecatl Metztli.** Tonatiuh is heat, radiance, light, strength, a warm and trustworthy character. When angry, the heat is overwhelming. As an important part of Quetzalcoatl, the Sun is balanced by the strength of the Moon, which manifests with Tezcatlipoca, activating intellect, curiosity, and sensitivity through the function of Metztli, and through Tecziztecatl, activating creativity and the regulation of cycles to generate new lives.

#### Seventh trecena, Ce Quiahuitl

Tlaloc and Chalchiuhtlicue are the companions in the Borbonicus Codex, while in the Tonalamatl of Aubin, Tlaloc is accompanied by Chicomecoatl. In the Borgia Codex, Tlaloc is the only ruler, and in the Telleriano Remensis he is accompanied by Tlazolteotl according to the commentary's caption, but according to the drawing, the attire is that of Tlazohteotl.

**Thirteen days, ce quiahuitl. Impulsive, tenacious when they set out to reach a goal; passive when they believe it is not worth struggling.**

**Companions, Tlaloc, Chicomecoatl, Chalchiuhtlicue, and Tlazohteotl.** Tlaloc symbolizes drive and forcefulness through the strength of the storm. Through Chalchiuhtlicue it has the same qualities as flowing water, which are fluidity, transparency, and tenacity. Through Chicomecoatl, fertility and sustenance. And through Tlazohteotl, the capacity to give affection.

#### Eighth trecena, Ce Malinalli

In the Telleriano, in the Tonalamatl of Aubin, and in Vaticanus Latinus, Mayahuel is the principal ruler of this trecena, accompanied by Centeotl, represented by a youthful-looking figure. In the Borbonicus the masculine figure changes without losing that appearance, except that here he is a young Tezcatlipoca. In the Borgia Codex he is also a young man, drinking a precious liquid.

**Thirteen days, ce malinalli. Evolving, changeable, self-critical.**

**Companions, Mayahuel, Centeotl, and Tezcatlipoca.** Through Mayahuel, the fertility of the Earth and the lunar phases are used to develop sensitivity. Mayahuel exists within the maguey as the feminine essence of the plant and of its sweet sap. She is a symbol of union, evolution, and the awakening of the senses to other levels of consciousness. Centeotl, in his other aspect related to plants, is Xochipilli. Here he joins with the young Tezcatlipoca to give direction to the manifestation of emotions and feelings through creativity.

#### Ninth trecena, Ce Coatl

All references in the amoxtin agree that the companions of this trecena are Xiuhtecuhtli and Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli. The first is the Lord of Turquoise, of the year, of celestial heat, and of the center. Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli is the Morning Star, the luminous Lord of the Dawn.

**Thirteen days, ce coatl. Sensitive, generous, their appearance renews itself cyclically; they influence other people mentally.** Coatl is vibration. The undulation of serpents represents the likewise undulating vibration of the force that emanates from the earth and manifests in absolutely everything that exists upon it.

**Companions, Xiuhtecuhtli and Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli.** Let us remember what Xiuhtecuhtli represents in human beings: centrality, justice, rectitude, and honesty. The Morning Star is luminosity that serves as a guide so as not to become lost along the paths of life.

#### Tenth trecena, Ce Tecpatl

All sources agree that the companions of this trecena are Tonatiuh, the bearer of heat and light, and Mictlantecuhtli, the Lord of the Place of Rest.

**Thirteen days, ce tecpatl.**

**Assurance, hardness, curiosity, intuition, active intellect.**

**Companions, Tonatiuh and Mictlantecuhtli.** Let us remember that Tonatiuh is heat, radiance, light, strength, a warm and trustworthy character; when angry, the heat is overwhelming. In this group of thirteen days he is accompanied by Mictlantecuhtli, who transforms and renews everything. Death is an inevitable passage to other planes of our existence within the whole, and as living beings we must not remain static. Many of the messages we receive from all this symbolism carry an implicit sense of constant transmutation.

#### Eleventh trecena, Ce Ozomahtli

In the Borgia Codex this trecena is ruled by Tlazohteotl, with her characteristic attire adorned with moons and a Moon nose ornament identical to Pahtecatl's, whose name literally means he of the healing plants, since pahtli is herbal medicine and pahtía is the verb to heal, to cure. In all the other amoxtin consulted, Pahtecatl is the companion of this group. In the Borgia, Tlazohteotl has before her an ocelotl with luminous skin and the symbol of beauty upon its forehead, holding a standard in one arm as a representation of transcendence through rigorous disciplines. In the other codices, with the same meaning, an eagle and an ocelot appear together with the reading cuauhtli-ocelotl, as a set of qualities that mean being bold and measured, daring and cautious, and the whole range of attributes that the orders of cuauhtli and ocelotl warriors had to develop.

**Thirteen days, ce ozomahtli. Sensuality, a pleasant and festive presence, ease of expression.**

**Companions, Pahtecatl and Tlazohteotl, with the qualities of Cuauhtli and Ocelotl.** Although the sources mention Pahtecatl only as a pulque deity associated with ome tochtli, the literal translation of his name contradicts this naive assessment. As we have already mentioned, this concept is closely related to the health of

people and, above all, to familiarity with and knowledge of healing plants. From this perspective, Pahtecatl is all people who have the quality of taking an interest in or becoming involved in the practice of health through natural medicine, which in turn is linked with Tlazohteotl, the Earth, who as a mother seeks the health of all her children. The qualities of Cuauhtli-ocelotl have already been described so that one may strive to be eagle and ocelotl in everyday life.

#### Twelfth trecena, Ce Cuetzpalin

Companion, Iztlacoliuhqui; there are no variants in any of the amoxtin consulted. In the Borgia Codex the image is enriched by the presence of Yayauhqui Tezcatlipoca, the dark one, reaffirming that Iztlacoliuhqui is part of the context of Tezcatlipoca.

**Thirteen days, ce cuetzpalin.**

**Intellectual fertility, creative, very realistic, and sexuality that manifests in every aspect of life.**

**Companion, Tezcatlipoca as Iztlacoliuhqui.** In numerous sources his name is written Itztlacoliuhqui, since iztli or itztli is used interchangeably as the name of obsidian. In the Tonalpohualli he symbolizes coldness and hardness. He is part of the awakening of the inner being in a constant refinement of what is worth retaining in our thoughts and attitudes.

#### Thirteenth trecena, Ce Ollin

Tlazohteotl as Ixcuina, or more correctly Ixcuinan, is the companion of One Movement. In the Vaticanus Latinus Codex, with Ixcuina is Tezcatlipoca wearing the attire of a bird called, according to most sources, uactli, or tototl uactli according to Remi Simeon's Dictionary, which announced with its song whether something was going to happen. In the other codices, instead of Tezcatlipoca, there is a large bird with attributes indeed belonging to this concept.

**Thirteen days, ce ollin. Restless, active, enterprising, in constant movement.**

### Printed page 186

**Companions, Tlazohteotl Ixcuinan and Tezcatlipoca as a bird.** Ixcuinan is a faculty of Tezcatlipoca that manifests in women of mature age. It is the capacity to look at the face and know what is happening to people, and always provide a solution and a good example. As Tlazohteotl it is knowing how to give affection to whoever needs it. Tezcatlipoca as an augural bird is also a quality of being able to predict and calculate future events through a mind alert to that type of exercise.

#### Fourteenth trecena, Ce Itzcuintli

Xipe Totec, the Lord Who Changes His Skin, is the ruler of this group. In the Borgia Codex he has before him a human being entering the serpentine knowledge of the vibration of everything that exists, from which one day he will emerge through the mouth of the serpent. In this codex its body is adorned with the malinalli plant and its seed heads, representing the evolving, cyclical, and changing process. In the other documents the serpent is adorned with leaves of this plant and feathers of the quetzal tototl, symbolizing a spiritual transformation.

**Thirteen days, ce itzcuintli.**

**Fidelity in its fullest expression; curious, travelers, and loyal to their people and their place of origin.**

**Companion, Xipe Totec.** As a cyclical concept of change in nature, this is how he manifests in people. It is a constant refinement of both internal and external attitudes that serve to rearrange and order life. The serpent is a discipline of learning, surely of the science of life, from which only those who succeed in transforming themselves inwardly toward a harmonious state of existence emerge.

#### Fifteenth trecena, Ce Calli

In the Vaticanus Latinus and Telleriano codices, the companion of this trecena is Itzpapalotl, Obsidian Butterfly, dressed as an enormous butterfly. In the Borbonicus, by contrast, she is an eagle woman, like the one described by the song of Quilaztli Chalmecacihuatl in the songs recovered by Sahagún's informants. In the Borgia Codex she is a woman with a fleshless face wearing eagle feathers, before a tree from which life springs, represented by the precious liquid that is blood, with beads of jade and turquoise.

### Printed page 187

##### Thirteen days, One House

Perseverance, protection, sustenance.

Companion, Itzpapalotl. Feminine energy that awakens from nightfall until the Sun rises again. It is intuition, sensitivity, and inner strength, manifested by Tezcatlipoca. Itzpapalotl is Tezcatlipoca, as are Ixcuinan and Ixnextli, but within the feminine context.

#### Sixteenth trecena, Ce Cozcacuauhtli

In all the codices that refer to the tonalli calendar, the companion of this trecena is Xolotl, the twin of the Morning Star. Xolotl is the Evening Star, and the translation is "the one who is a complement." In the Borgia Codex he faces the glyph of an Earth movement with four bands of magnitude, and the coral snake, which also represents the Earth, is broken into several pieces. In the Borbonicus and the Tonalamatl of Aubin, Xolotl is in a frame of water waves with Tlaloc carrying a Sun disk. In the Tellerianus and the Vaticanus Latinus, Tlaltecuhtli is eating Tonatiuh, who wears a Tlaloc mask. Del Paso y Troncoso calls this action Tlalchitonatiuh, the Sun at ground level, at the moment of twilight when it hides each day so that the Evening Star may be seen during its period behind the Sun. Subsequently, all authors have repeated the name Tlachitonatiuh, applying it to this peculiar Tlaloc who overlaps Tonatiuh as a joint action of twilight rains that allow the sunset to be seen.

##### Thirteen days, One Vulture

Practical, timely, calm, calculating; they know how to make the most of materials and situations.

Companion, Xolotl, with Tlaloc and Tonatiuh. Xolotl is the ideal companion; these are participative, helpful, and selfless people. The conjunction of Tlaloc with Tonatiuh is the same one we find in the elements of heat that harmonize with those of water. The result is daily evaluation as night arrives, when teotlac, the afternoon, brings us peacefully closer to the dual Generating Principle called Ometeotl through the warmth of the Sun and the wet Earth that invite reflection.

Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli and Xolotl, with their very different radiation and their periods of visibility and invisibility alternating in recurring cycles, were very important in the ancient science of the Quetzalcoatl for the study of life on Earth. In the codices they wear insignia that relate them to the science of life, Quetzalcoatl, such as the spiral conch-shell disk and the curved ear ornament.

##### Thirteen days, One Wind

Communicator, sower, changeable.

Companion, Chantihco. Represents warmth in daily dealings, affection, and strength that instills security in those close to them.

#### Nineteenth trecena, Ce Cuauhtli

Xochiquetzalli is the ruler of these thirteen days, accompanied in the Borbonicus and the Aubin by a mammal with colored spots, without specifying what animal it is, although the commentary in the Fondo de Cultura Económica edition mentions that it resembles a skunk and relates it to Tezcatlipoca. In the Tellerianus and the Vaticanus Latinus, the little colored animal is covered by its attire of quezaltototl, the beautiful bird; in these documents there are colored centipedes and snakes. Xochiquetzalli in the Borgia Codex is adorned with a cipactli copilli topped by the symbol of beauty, a plume of quetzal feathers or leaves of the malinalli plant with a yellow spike, with a large gold mirror. Before her is a young Tezcatlipoca with a gold mirror as a breast ornament bordered by tongues of fire.

##### Thirteen days, One Eagle

Boldness, precision, bearing. Very high flight that makes one stand out, and splendor in attitudes.

Companions, Xochiquetzalli and Tezcatlipoca. Xochiquetzalli, as the representative of beauty in everything that exists, accompanied by Tezcatlipoca, is the constant purification of our negative attitudes that may harm other people. She also represents constant physical purification through exercise and sport, contained in the drawings of the ballcourt, and mental purification through patolli and its enormous beans to one side, as the means of always having health and a clear mind in daily work upon the Earth represented by cipactli.

#### Twentieth trecena, Ce Tochtli

In all the codices consulted, the rulers are Xiuhtecuhtli and Itztapaltotec, with the exception of the Borgia, in which Xipe Totec holds a torch instead of his staff, which in the other cases is in Itztapaltotec's hand.

##### Thirteen days, One Rabbit

Agile mind, ingenious, fruitful in ideas for overcoming obstacles.
