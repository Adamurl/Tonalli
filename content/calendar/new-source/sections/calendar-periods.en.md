## Full English translation

### Printed page 166

As we have stated elsewhere in this study, combining the sign and the number, with the full load of characteristics we know up to this point, results in 260 variants.

From this moment on, we shall begin recognizing the qualities and influences implicit in any date one wishes to analyze. For example, the characteristics of the numbers, without the companions we have studied, extend to the four year bearers. Knowing the characteristics of *tochtli*, *acatl*, *tecpatl*, and *calli*, drawing them from the preceding description of the 20 day signs, they can be combined with the qualities of the thirteen numerals.

As we have already seen, the companions of the numbers are personifications of qualities, characteristics, and influences of the elements acting in nature to regulate the cycles within which human life is immersed.

Some of them act only as companions or rulers in certain cases. Others, however, remain active, granting their influence during other periods of time.

### Printed page 167

| Time periods | Number of elements | Duration of each period |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rulers of the years | 7 | 52 years |
| Companions of the 18 twenty-day periods | 10 | 360 days |
| Companions of the 20 signs | 20 | 20 days |
| Lords of the thirteen-day periods | 20 | 260 days |
| Lords of the days | 13 | 13 days |
| Lords of the night | 9 | 9 days |

They also serve as rulers of the twenty fractions into which a day is divided for the calculation of the *tonalpohualli*.

Next we shall locate all the figures through which the elements of nature were represented as energetic entities holding an important place in the calendrical system, and what they confer on human beings in the so-called count of destinies from the perspective of Indigenous knowledge, which most often does not coincide with the parameters we use from the perspective of Western culture.

In these descriptions we shall try to avoid the forms of expression of modern culture's horoscopes, while also trying, when using the description of each figure, to take into account the importance of the space in which they are inscribed. The ruler of a year, whose influence is very subtle and diluted across 365 days, is not the same as that same companion as Lord of the Night, Yohualtecuhtli, whose influence relates to the development and psychological projection of a person from the subconscious, or, in another case, as Lord of the Day, Tonaltecuhtli, who directly influences the personality nourished principally by the *tonalli* of the environment and by what all living beings gather from nature in countless forms of energy.

The generating Principle of the energy that encompasses everything and is in everything is a dual principle formed of feminine parts and masculine parts, always in exact balance in space, distance, order, and number, called Ometeotl. From it come absolutely all things, the natural elements and the beings filling the space of creation called Omeyocan, the place of dual essence that is in the heavens, on Earth, and everywhere.

Everything that comes from Ometeotl, filling Omeyocan, to which Ilhuicaxicalli, the Celestial Bowl, and Tlalticpac or Ixtlalticpac, the face of the Earth, belong, is formed of dual matter and energy.

### Printed page 168

Let us recall what we have already set out on previous pages. In ancient times, by recording the elements of nature in human form, they left us a legacy of information in the *amoxtin* and in the pre-Hispanic sculptures of different periods and peoples. This leads us to consider that our ancestors synthesized the energetic influence received from each of them in a hieroglyphic and mathematical language of signs and drawings framing and clothing the figure of a human being. In this way we can read, for example, the descriptive qualities of a figure who carries half a Sun on his back, a gold pectoral on his chest, yellow hair, a *copilli* or headdress with eagle feathers, a *chimalli* or shield also of gold, and some arrows in one hand, besides other attributes he may carry according to the situation being described. Undoubtedly this is Tonatiuh, who is literally called the one who carries heat, or Tonalteotl, the generator of *tonalli*, as he is known in some communities of Mexican-language speakers. Independently of what the Sun is in nature, as the axis responsible for climatic changes and for the state of life's cycles in general, in this work we are interested in describing how a person related to this element behaves when, from birth, they are joined to the Sun if they have it as a companion in any of the forms we have already listed.

*Tonatiuh. Plate 71, Borgia Codex.*

Another graphic example would be the case of a female figure with the *copilli* or headdress made of a cotton band with one or two spindle whorls, winding the already twisted thread on a spindle, and small moons in her nose ornament or painted on her skirt. This is the representation of the Earth as a loving mother who provides everything for her children with affection, spinning and weaving life according to the Moon's phases. She is Tlazohteotl, a name that is not recorded in the sources but exists in everyday speech. Tlazohteotl, generator of affection, is one of the representations of the Earth as a feminine energy that grants human beings the gift of protection and excessive affection toward those they love. The same figure, but with different attire, describes another function of the Earth by changing a single sound in her name: Tlazolteotl. This female figure regularly has a butterfly-shaped bone on her skirt, or two crossed bones, representing the function of gathering into her bosom everything that has completed its life cycle, transforming organic matter into new living forms. A person linked by the calendrical system with Tlazolteotl acts by transforming their surroundings positively, generating ideas and helping loved ones in a constant resurgence, characteristics similar to those of Mictlancihuatl, a representation of the change that effectively takes place in Tlazolteotl's skirt.

*Tlazohteotl. Borbónico Codex.*
