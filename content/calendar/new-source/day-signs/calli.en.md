## Full English translation

### Printed page 117

#### Calli

Calli is generally translated as house, in the sense of a building or enclosure, as distinct from the place where one lives or that serves as a dwelling, which is called chantli in the Nahuatl language.

In Indigenous thought, the following is said:

> ...Calli is that which protects or shelters something, for example: acalli, a boat; huacalli, a slatted box that contains something; teocalli, where knowledge is kept; xicalli, a gourd bowl, a vessel that holds something at its center, such as flowers, sweets, and fruit in offerings. It may be the place where we work or where we go to study.

*Calli. Plate 6, Nuttall Codex.*

Metaphorically, calli is oneself as the vessel of one's own essence, for which one is solely responsible in life.

Cincalco, cen, one, calli, enclosure, and co, place, is another name for Temoanchan or Tamoanchan. It is the place of the primordial house, a metaphorical name for the great womb of Mother Earth, which, fertilized with water, the precious and vital liquid, gives birth to all beings, providing them with sustenance, protection, and shelter.

Those born under the calli sign are creative, home-loving, protective beings, much inclined to enjoy their family's company.

*Calli. Sun Stone.*

They are studious, hardworking, very provident, and somewhat sedentary people who seek to obtain sufficient means to avoid deprivation.
