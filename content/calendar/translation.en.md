#### Characteristics of the day signs

##### Cipactli

The first of the day signs is cipactli. The months and years of the Mexican calendar begin with this sign. In field information from places where caimans abound, the following is said of this species:

> ...the caiman is so strong that no other animal can defeat it; it lives both in the water and on land; it is one of the first animals that existed; its backbone can bear a great deal of weight; its hide, the skin, resembles the earth when we have already sown it and banked up the soil in the maize field.

*Cipactli. Plate 75, Nuttall Codex.*

In the chronicles of the sixteenth century, its name is translated not only as caiman but also as swordfish, fish, or serpent's head.

Its name consists of the roots ce, one; icpac, on, in, above; and the syllable tli, which defines it as a noun. The literal translation is "upon the first."

Cipactli is the first thing that man's ancestral memory records on Earth. It takes us back to dark and distant times. It is probably a representation of the great saurians that populated the world, which, originally aquatic species, emerged from the water to change into terrestrial beings. This explains the variations found in its representation, in which it may equally be a fish or a lizard like the alligator gar of the southeast.

Historical sources remind us that the place of origin called Tamoanchan is also the place of turquoise-blue mist and rain, Xiuhayauhtitlan, where life originates. It is precisely in swamps and marshes that the first forms of life appear, until geological ages later life emerges and appears upon the earth.

Cipactli is that beginning, as well as representing the Earth in general in most pre-Hispanic amoxtin.

The fangs of cipactli are represented by the sign tecpatl, a flint knife, which symbolizes the first light of knowledge recorded in men's genetic memory, through which they came to understand the formation of the universe, the formation of our world, and the origin of life.

In the tonalpohualli, the count of destinies, the characteristics of oral tradition are applied, in which cipactli is considered a beginning or a sign of leading people. They have an energetic character that gives them the gift of command.

In the book Ita Andehui, by Manuel López Grácida and Mariano López Ruiz, published by Julián S. Soto in Oaxaca in 1906, they refer to this sign as follows:

> Cipactli always appears as a fantastic being... it emerges from the waters in a shape resembling that of a crocodile... it brings with it the idea of beginning, commencement, origin. It enters into the formation of the name Cipactonal, in whom lies the beginning of the days, of the Sun, and of light.

Those born on a cipactli day are said to be enterprising, tenacious people of firm principles, since their burden, i mamal, carries great responsibility. They are beings who have roots, principles, and responsibility in life.

*Cipactli. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Ehecatl

It translates as air or wind. It is the first vital nourishment man receives at birth and cannot do without while alive. The figure of ehecatl is an abstract sign, represented by a human head whose face is covered by a mask with an elongated beak, suggesting the force with which the wind blows.

*Ehecatl. Plate 56, Borgia Codex.*

Oral information says that ehecatl...

> ...sometimes blows very strongly, sometimes very softly; when it is very hot, it is because he is asleep; he likes to carry clouds from one place to another; in the dry season he carries the seeds of withered plants to other places, which is why they call him the sower; he is the one who makes sounds audible, because without him words, songs, birdsong, and thunder would not be heard; when he joins with the rain, it may be a very gentle breeze or a strong storm.

Expressed another way, it is as follows:

Ehecatl, as part of Quetzalcoatl, the science of life, is nature's ideal vehicle for moving clouds from one place to another so that they release water over cultivated fields.

He is metaphorically called the sower because, besides sweeping the planted fields before the rain falls, he scatters the seeds of the plants that cover the earth year after year. Men depend on ehecatl to exist and to communicate with one another. He is the means of life and expression for all nature, because he gathers and carries every sound.

Without ehecatl there would be no music or harmony in the song of water, birds, trees, and men. Without ehecatl everything would be silent. Ehecatl produces his own sounds, from the faint hiss of the breeze to the roar of hurricane winds.

Those born on an ehecatl day are attributed a changeable character that can range from peaceful placidity to a fury difficult to contain. This tendency toward extremes must be centered by channeling its qualities toward constant creativity, since ehecatl, as the breath of life, is the ihiyotl that also contains the inspiration of the spirit, manifested in ideas that can take material form in many expressions through the arts. Another of its qualities is ease of expression and harmony in singing.

*Ehecatl. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Calli

Calli is generally translated as house, in the sense of a building or enclosure, as distinct from the place where one lives or that serves as a dwelling, which is called chantli in the Nahuatl language.

In Indigenous thought, the following is said:

> ...Calli is that which protects or shelters something, for example: acalli, a boat; huacalli, a slatted box that contains something; teocalli, where knowledge is kept; xicalli, a gourd bowl, a vessel that holds something at its center, such as flowers, sweets, and fruit in offerings. It may be the place where we work or where we go to study.

*Calli. Plate 6, Nuttall Codex.*

Metaphorically, calli is oneself as the vessel of one's own essence, for which one is solely responsible in life.

Cincalco, cen, one, calli, enclosure, and co, place, is another name for Temoanchan or Tamoanchan. It is the place of the primordial house, a metaphorical name for the great womb of Mother Earth, which, fertilized with water, the precious and vital liquid, gives birth to all beings, providing them with sustenance, protection, and shelter.

Those born under the calli sign are creative, home-loving, protective beings, much inclined to enjoy their family's company.

*Calli. Sun Stone.*

They are studious, hardworking, very provident, and somewhat sedentary people who seek to obtain sufficient means to avoid deprivation.

#### Cuetzpalin

Cuetzpalli or cuetzpalin is the generic name of certain species of small reptiles that we know as lizards.

It is said of them that...

> ...they always stay close to the ground; most are very fast when they run; when they are still, pressing their bellies against the earth, they rise and fall constantly; there are slender lizards and thicker ones; iguanas are the largest of all.

*Cuetzpalli. Plate 20, Borgia Codex.*

In the Mexican language, the iguana is called acuecuetzpalin or cuauhcuetzpalli, a reptile between the larger lizards and the small lizards, which in appearance resembles a small version of an enormous prehistoric iguanodon.

Its Nahuatl name translates as tree lizard or lizard of the trees that grow beside water. Like the small cuetzpalli, they are associated with the fertility of the earth and the sexuality of human beings. In the Borgia Codex, the sign cuetzpalli is linked to the male sexual organ of Xochipilli and Tezcatlipoca.

Jacinto de la Serna merely confirms the name, saying:

> The fourth day is cuetzpallin, which was depicted by a lizard or little lizard.

Of those born on a cuetzpalli day it is said:

> ...that they would always have abundant lineage and descendants.

All this is so provided the other elements of the tonalpohualli reinforce the sign, since otherwise they are sterile, but with a great capacity for sexuality. Perhaps because of this characteristic, chroniclers such as Sahagún and Durán classified cuetzpalli as a "bad sign," for in it they saw the instinct of human sexuality as diabolical. The potential of sexuality can be used to increase sensory possibilities beyond the customary ones, generating the inner strength needed to undertake everyday tasks with determination.

*Cuetzpalin. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Coatl

Coatl means serpent, and it is the generic name for reptiles such as boas, vipers, and snakes.

*Coatl. Plate 65, Borgia Codex.*

In the countryside it is said that:

> Snakes sense people and animals; they know where they are even in darkness; they say they sense with their foreheads; they put little animals to sleep when they stare at them; when they run, their whole body undulates; they feel with all their skin and shed it regularly; they say that if we learned truly to see, we would see the light coming out of trees, rocks, and people, undulating like little snakes.

With these simple opinions we can define the sign coatl as the manifestation of warm and luminous energy given off by the elements and by all beings that exist in nature, accepting thereby that it is a symbol of the precious energy of life. Whoever perceives it is a tlamatini whose perception is heightened, and therefore a wise man within true knowledge.

Coatl also represents Mother Earth's fertility cycles, which interweave their energy in the skirt of Tonantzin Coatlicue in mathematical cycles related to one another, generating the different species that live upon her.

The two-headed serpent, so familiar in drawings and sculptures, is the fusion of the two cosmic forces that generate everything in existence, opposing forces that at the same time complement each other so that space and time may exist. Within that context are matter, activity, and life.

The adornments in its representations are generally feathers, jewels, or rattles, depending on whether the reference is to the terrestrial, aquatic, aerial, or celestial.

Tezcatlipoca and Quetzalcoatl, in manifesting as pure energy, were represented as two great serpents. In the well-known narrative in André Thevet's book Hystoire du Mexique, the two great serpents divide Tlaltecuhtli, Lord of the Earth, in two, and with one of his parts form the terrestrial surface, identified as Oxomoco, and the atmospheric envelope as Cipactonal. In both, the heat coming from the great celestial Bowl accumulates, nourishing all the elements necessary for life to exist.

*Coatl. Stone of the Suns.*

Of those born on a day of the sign coatl, according to the tonalpohualli, it is said that they are very sensitive, generous people who do not like to hoard anything. They like to change their personal appearance or the appearance of their dwelling frequently. They have the ability to communicate through thought with those who vibrate at their same frequency. They can easily intensify their thoughts, concentrating to the point of influencing other people. The sensitivity of their skin helps them perceive atmospheric changes. They have such alert intelligence that for many people it is a symbol of wisdom.

The mentality of the European chroniclers, who saw the serpent as the Judeo-Christian symbol of original sin, led them to classify coatl as an evil sign.

#### Miquiztli

It literally means death.

In non-Christian Indigenous thought, it represents only a necessary passage to a new state of life, like a transmutation into something that cannot be defined:

> We all have to die; the grandparents say that death is only a change; it is as if death stopped us for a moment so that we could then begin again; in life, as with plants, everything dies and is reborn once more after resting for a time.

*Miquiztli. Plate 26, Borgia Codex.*

What was frightening to the religious was, for Indigenous peoples, part of the naturalness of life. Death, from this perspective, is a transformation after a period of rest.

In nature there is always a span of stillness before a new manifestation of life. For seeds to germinate, they must have rested for a period of time; for the earth to be fertilized, it must have rested throughout the winter.

Miquiztli, in our daily life, is restorative sleep, the moment of concentration before creativity manifests itself. It is a regenerative interval before undertaking an activity. It also represents convalescence in illness; it is the bridge between menstruation and ovulation that allows new beings to be conceived. From life comes death, and from death comes life. In that sense, miquiztli and malinalli, annual grass, are related, since both represent nature's perpetual renewal.

*Miquiztli. Stone of the Suns.*

Fear of death caused the European chroniclers to classify miquiztli as a sad, melancholy, or evil sign, but for the native peoples its meaning is different. For those born on a miquiztli day, their characteristics are reflection, appraisal, and evaluation before undertaking any activity. But once they have begun something, nothing stops them; they are daring until they achieve their ends. They do not like to talk about themselves and are said to be introverted, very closed within themselves. An excellent quality is that, through well-judged suggestions, they can influence other people to transform habits in ways that always result in what is most appropriate and positive for each case.

#### Mazatl

Mazatl is the generic name for the different species of deer that inhabit Mexico. Of mazatl it is said that:

> They are always attentive to what happens nearby; they are very nervous and have very keen hearing; they feel with their skin and are said to hear with their hooves; they run very fast through the brush and do not like to live in captivity.

*Mazatl. Plate 19, Borgia Codex.*

It is an animal of beautiful bearing, an agile and slender body, a restless nature, and great perceptiveness. With very keen hearing, it is always attentive to the sounds carried by the wind. It lives in the mountains, in constant relation to freedom in nature, becomes active with the Sun's energy, and is sensitive to changes in humidity brought by rain.

In symbolism, mazatl is related to the Sun, heat, and fire. It is therefore identified as a solar animal, as are some birds and certain insects, such as the hummingbird and butterfly. In the Borgia Codex it appears on plate 53, representing Xochipilli as the Sun's fertilizing energy, with the twenty day signs and a butterfly drawn on its face.

Durán says of the sign mazatl:

> ...that those born under it were restless and never stayed in one place.

Sahagún says only that it is a good sign, without going into detail. In the countryside they still say that those whose attitudes are related to deer are nervous and restless and always attentive to what happens around them. They have good hearing and are sensitive to changes in the weather, such as heat, wind, and the moisture of rain. They like open spaces and freedom and prefer to live in the countryside, since in cities they feel imprisoned. They are very affectionate, sometimes even sacrificing themselves for those they love.

#### Tochtli

Tochtli is the rabbit, a symbol of the fertility of the Earth and of beings governed by the phases of the Moon. Of rabbits it is said in the countryside that:

> Rabbits are very clever in the wild; they are fast and know how to look after themselves; they have a reputation for intelligence, and for that reason it is said that they are always plotting how to make fools of the coyotes and small wildcats that want to eat them. There are several stories among our people about that reputation. The females often have young; they take as long to be born as the Moon takes to go from full to disappearing and becoming full again. An old story says that a rabbit was imprinted on the face of the Moon; perhaps that is why these stories mention that if the Sun is an eagle, the Moon is a rabbit.

*Tochtli. Plate 20, Borgia Codex.*

In familiar language, Tochtli, because of most of its characteristics, is a distinctly lunar animal. Its period of procreation lasts an average of 29 days, the duration of a complete lunar period.

According to the Legend of the Suns, its figure is imprinted on the face of the Moon and is perfectly visible at full moon. In the amoxtin, the books of the ancient Mexicans, the Moon is drawn with the outline of a pot-shaped vessel formed by a curved bone, and inside it a rabbit surrounded by the glyph of atl, water.

The lunar influence that regulates all beings' cycles with respect to periods of fertilization is generally known, as is its great influence on all the liquids in nature, such as plant sap, animal blood, the rise and fall of water in large lagoons, and the tides of the oceans, influencing the regulation or imbalance of brain activity.

Because of its great capacity for procreation, tochtli has been considered a symbol of pleasure, not only sexual pleasure but everything agreeable in life. Pleasure must be enjoyed in the proper measure, because if it reaches excess, it is then considered a vice.

#### Atl

The ninth day sign is atl, water. We gathered some very interesting information about this natural element:

> Water is the river, the irrigation channel, the lagoon; it is in the well and flowing from the spring. When it is in the water jar, it has the shape of the water jar; it also takes the shape of the river, the pitcher, or the irrigation channel. If something absorbs it, this happens slowly, without haste; thus earth, sand, and cotton drink it up. It refreshes us when we are thirsty, rejuvenates us and makes us feel lighter after bathing. When it is very still and the air does not move it, it even looks like a mirror. In streams, running among the stones, it murmurs and sings. Turned into rain, it moistens the hills and fields so that plants sprout. With the water of the irrigation channels we water the crops. In the swollen river it roars like an animal. A stream of water falling constantly can make a hole in rocks.

*Atl. Plate 18, Borgia Codex.*

The opinions gathered about both ehecatl, wind, and atl, water, have been varied and rich. Translated into ordinary language, we may say that atl represents everything that adapts or molds itself to circumstances, just as water adopts the shape of the vessel that contains it.

Water is the element of fertility which, acting in duality with the heat that comes from above, the precious fire, produces life in all its forms on Earth.

For pre-Hispanic peoples, the sign atl is very important. Sacred would be the appropriate adjective, because it is the primordial element of life, since water makes up the greater part of the organic constitution of all living beings.

For human beings, atl is the vital liquid with which they quench their thirst, purify and wash their bodies, cleanse and rejuvenate themselves, and which in some places even serves as a route for travel in boats.

The seas, rivers, lakes, and lagoons are mirrors of water, the precious jewels with which nature adorns the skirt of Tonantzin, our Mother Earth.

In the codices, water is represented by a fret, figuratively made to undulate by the wind. If the fret is red, it represents blood, the precious water of life that animates our being. When water joins fire, it becomes atlachinolli, a symbol of the strength of the warrior we should all be, the warrior of life who undertakes the struggle of daily work regardless of the obstacles to overcome.

Part of the work of the ancient wise men of the rank of Quetzalcoatl was the study and use of running waters, taking into account the force or energy emanating from them.

The influence received by those born on an atl day is strength and patience, like the drop of water that bores through a rock or the devastating force of a torrent. They also have an aptitude for adapting to life's different circumstances, the quality of water that molds itself to the vessel containing it. They know how to be transparent in their actions and take the opportunity to enter others' minds through subtle suggestion, just as water is absorbed through moisture.

*Atl. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Itzcuintli

Itzcuintli is a variety of dog of pre-Hispanic origin. Of them it is said that:

> There are hardly any of the little dogs of old left; they may have behaved like today's dogs, which are faithful and loyal to the people they love. They accompany them into the hills and to the planting, and women to the river to wash and when they fetch water. They look after them and always defend them against other animals or when they are in danger. There are times when they take to leaving home for a while, but they always return. The females attract many dogs when they are in heat; the males are playful when young and very quarrelsome when grown.

*Itzcuintli. Plate 13, Borgia Codex.*

Historical sources say that itzcuintli was a food much appreciated by demanding palates. Today few specimens remain in some parts of our country, for example in the state of Nayarit. Representations of fattened little dogs in the cultures of the West are famous.

An unsurpassed playmate for children because it is playful and amusing, it was an excellent guardian of houses because of its loyalty. A wanderer by nature, it always returns happily home, a loyal and faithful companion of men. Chichi is the generic noun for the different species of dogs, of which itzcuintli is only one variant. There are other varieties such as techichi, tepezcuintli, and probably the one we know as chihuahueño.

In the amoxtin, the codices, they are generally represented with gray fur and a dark patch around the eye; in other cases their bodies are white with black spots.

> As we have already mentioned, oral tradition affirms that itzcuintli represents fidelity, loyalty toward those it loves, and a passion for discovering foreign lands without losing its connection with its own homeland.

In mythology concerning death, a reddish-yellow itzcuintli accompanies the dead on their journey to Mictlan, the place of rest and stillness. When someone died, they were buried accompanied by their faithful little dog.

For Friar Bernardino de Sahagún, itzcuintli is simply a "good" sign. For Durán it is a sign of generous people, "fond of being asked for favors and granting them."

*Itzcuintli. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Ozomahtli

Ozomahtli in the Mexican language is the generic name for all species of monkeys known in Mexico. The most representative is the species known as the spider monkey, which was brought to the highlands by caravans of merchants, the pochtecah. The inhabitants of cities such as Tenochtitlan and Tetzcuco liked to keep them in their houses.

In places where monkeys are plentiful today, the following is said of them:

> Monkeys are agile and amusing as they move among the branches of trees; they even seem like people in the way they behave; they make a great commotion when many monkeys gather.

*Ozomahtli. Plate 13, Borgia Codex.*

The grace and agility of ozomahtli's movements were possibly the reason it has been associated with amusement and joy since time immemorial.

Ozomahtli represents feminine sensuality and is related to hormonal activity, which manifests itself cyclically and makes a woman exceedingly graceful, cheerful, and sensual as a prelude to the sexual act.

Those born on an ozomahtli day, men or women, are cheerful, entertaining, and very witty people, although in some things they are somewhat superficial. Great sensuality is evident in them. In Durán's chronicle we read the following:

#### Malinalli

It is the twelfth day sign. It represents everything that is transformed through constant rebirth.

> We know this plant as charcoal-maker's grass. There are several kinds of this plant. Some have thin leaves and others have broader, more abundant leaves. Its flowering spike is brown in some and white in royal malinalli. These blossoms are called malinalxochitl. It is there all the time, except that in the rainy season it is very green, making the hills turn green. In the past, bundles for charcoal were made with it, twisting it like ropes and interweaving it like tule or palm leaves.

*Malinalli. Plate 56, Borgia Codex.*

Malinalli means that which tangles or twists. It comes from the verb malina, which means to twist. In the chronicles it has been given the meaning of thicket, climbing plant, braid, broom, or simply grass, as one more of the annual plants that cover Mother Earth in greenery each year.

Malinalli is represented in drawings as a plant with elongated leaves and a yellow flowering spike, identified with malinalzacatl or malinalxihuitl, with straw-colored or whitish blooms. The leaves of these plants can be braided, interwoven, or twisted, giving rise to a great variety of objects for everyday use.

In some hieroglyphs the plant sprouts from a fleshless jaw adorned with cempoalxochitl flowers, and in other cases the jaw alone represents the malinalli day.

The function of the malinalli sign is similar to that of the miquiztli sign because both refer to constant renewal. Let us remember that miquiztli is constant and necessary renewal through rest. Malinalli is that which changes and renews itself cyclically, like grass that dries up and disappears, becoming part of the Earth, only to appear again and fill a new cycle with greenery.

On this subject Father Durán says:

> ...in imitation of the thicket that dries up each year and then turns green again.

It is a symbol of constant evolution. Those born on a malinalli day have a natural inclination to learn about and study the medicinal plants that serve to heal human beings. They also like to renew themselves continually through the self-evaluation of their conduct and self-criticism as a result of their own inner knowledge.

*Malinalli. Plate 19, Borgia Codex.*

#### Acatl

Acatl means reed. Like tule, it is a plant that grows abundantly in damp places. It is very useful in the countryside. The information gathered about the reed is as follows:

> Reeds are strong and very straight. We make flutes with them, and thin ones serve to make arrows. Thicker ones serve to make tlacochtli, spears one armspan long. Otate is like acatl, except that it is not hollow. With it, fire can be drawn from a soft little stick by spinning it rapidly on its tip with the hands.

*Acatl. Plate 12, Borgia Codex.*

Since the sixteenth century acatl has been wrongly translated as cane. In the Mexican language ohuatl means cane. Ohuatl are the jointed, solid, juicy stalks, such as those of maize and sugarcane.

Within the symbolism of the amoxtin, acatl represents the heat emanating from the Sun, called tonaacatl, whose function is to reach the earth with the fertilization necessary to produce sustenance.

### Page 128

In pre-Hispanic cosmogony, acatl is an important element as part of the calendrical names of the two concepts that form nature, called Ce-acatl, Quetzalcoatl, and Ome-acatl, Tezcatlipoca. These in turn, when demythologized, represent the bioenergy possessed by living beings, as organic energy and as the essence of the spirit. In the Mexica codices, acatl is represented by a clump of reeds sprouting from the bottom of a water channel, while in amoxtin of Mixtec tradition and style it is a bundle of reeds with arrowheads, depicting darts that, when thrown, convey the idea of speed, strength, direction, and consequently purposefulness.

In the hieroglyphs representing the lighting of a ceremonial fire, including the New Fire ceremony, as a bioenergetic cycle of Tezcatlipoca in Ome-acatl years, the reed is the vertical element that turns upon a horizontal piece of wood, which is ignited through friction. Acatl is generally adorned with eagle down or cotton tassels.

Those born on an acatl day have a very alert intelligence, eager to know and analyze in depth everything that interests them. They observe and analyze everything dispassionately. They always keep their objectives in mind. They are centered and not very emotional people, very frank in their dealings. They radiate confidence, although they are not very affectionate.

Fray Diego Durán says of those born on an acatl day:

> ...as the cane is hollow and without a heart, so those born under this sign were heartless men...

*Acatl. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Ocelotl

Ocelotl has incorrectly been translated as tiger or jaguar. It is a kind of feline abundant in Mexico that we now know as the ocelot. In areas where animals of this species still survive, we heard the following:

> The ocelot or little tiger is a cautious animal. When it searches for its prey, it walks very quietly and attentively. When it finds it, it carefully judges the distance, its size, and the risk. Once certain, it pounces upon it and never misses. They say the only animal that defeats it is the caiman. Because of the color and sheen of its skin, it always draws attention. They call it xochimiztli, flowering puma, because the spots of its coat look like flowers.

*Ocelotl. Plate 66, Nuttall Codex.*

Agile, slender, and beautiful in appearance, it is a hunter by nature, cautious when approaching its prey, and when it attacks it does so with such determination that no obstacle matters to it. Its skin is tawny yellow with bands or rosettes of an intense black, which is why it is also known as xochimiztli, meaning flowering feline. In human beings it symbolizes the successful accomplishment of everything undertaken. There is a flowering in this: the prey is a goal attained, and each achievement becomes a new flower in the beautiful coat.

In ancient times ocelotl was the nagual with which the Olmecs identified themselves, as a people who were the center from which culture radiated throughout Anahuac.

Within what we call the science of Tezcatlipoca, ocelotl also has two apparently opposing symbolic meanings.

The first represents the night sky filled with stars, the bowl of the sky, so close and yet so distant, which human beings contemplate from the earth.

The second refers to the inner parts of the Earth, such as Tepeyolohtli, Heart of the Mountains, with forests, hollows, and caves, places where the ocelot lives. In the codices Tepeyolohtli is represented by an ocelotl.

Ocelotl is also the symbol of one of the Earth's geological ages, called Ocelotonatiuh, Sun of Tiger, according to the Legend of the Suns.

The ocelot warriors, whose true name is oceloyaoquizqueh, together with the eagle warriors, the cuauhyaoquizqueh, were the most important orders of the Yaoquizcayotl, responsible for the goods and important persons in the government of Anahuac.

Those born on an ocelotl day have a beautiful presence, especially women. They are cautious in their plans, tenacious and determined in carrying them out. They carefully calculate the likelihood of success, their capabilities and limitations. When completely certain, they act. They are people who can be trusted, pleasant and affectionate. They have a gift for command and the ability to guide other people. They are exceedingly responsible and notable. In a crowd they always stand out, unable to avoid it.

Some characteristics of cipactli recur in the ocelotl sign because it is the first sign after the first count of thirteen days.

*Ocelotl. Stone of the Suns.*

#### Cuauhtli

The fifteenth day sign is cuauhtli, the eagle. Concerning these birds, we gathered the following opinions:

> The eagle flies very high. It is beautiful. It is strong. It likes to be free. It has very good eyesight: from above it can see small animals. It is impulsive, attacks suddenly, and always catches its prey. It has a very penetrating gaze, even frightening. We like it as an emblem. They say it can look steadily at the Sun without hurting its eyes. The grandparents say the eagle is the Sun, because the Sun is a gold-colored eagle.

*Cuauhtli. Nuttall Codex.*

Because of all these attributes, the eagle represents the Sun. It is the nagual of Tonatiuh in full maturity when the zenith passages occur in our geographical region, with the Sun in the full manifestation of strength and energy. In present-day terms, we would say that because of all these characteristics, the eagle is a symbol of those who stand above others, reaching the heights, loving freedom, and possessing splendor and majestic bearing.

Cuauhtli is the eagle, mistress of the vast expanses. It nests in steep mountains. To locate its prey from the heights it possesses highly developed visual acuity. It is daring, sometimes acting more on impulse than reflection. It is the only animal that can look directly at the Sun without affecting its sight.

The beautiful plumage of the golden eagle and the royal eagle forms part of the attire of the young people who in ancient times attained the distinction of being cuauhyaoquizqueh, warriors of the Sun, on the same level as the oceloyaoquizqueh, ocelot warriors. These two orders were the guardians of what was precious in Anahuac societies.

The eagle has been the symbol representing Mexico in the national coat of arms, as well as all the peoples of the Uto-Aztecan linguistic family.

Fray Diego Durán writes about cuauhtli:

> This next sign, which is cuauhtli, meaning eagle, has the same properties already described for the tiger sign, except that they add that one born under this sign, besides having the said properties of the tiger, had others...

Of those born under the cuauhtli sign, it is also said that they are tenacious, impulsive, and determined. They possess keen vision for accomplishing their aims. They like high flight and love freedom of action in unlimited spaces. Their expression is serious. They are intelligent and determined.

#### Ollin

Ollin is movement, the constant activity that exists in everything, because nothing is static, nothing is motionless in nature. Gathering opinions in the countryside about what movement is proved somewhat difficult. As we have realized, the answers are formulated according to people's experiences. When they express opinions about something they have not witnessed, they always mention: it is what the elders say.

> Olinía is to make everything move, whether by running or working. It is being in the river, moving against the current. Ollin is being alive. The elders say that what is at rest, still, is so because it is dead. But everything that has life will always be in motion and making noise.

*Ollin. Plate 21, Borgia Codex.*

Our view of what ollin, movement, is encompasses everything in nature. Throughout our comprehensible universe there is activity. Even in what we know as static there is movement. Let us look at some examples.

In living beings, physically there is activity in all their organs, tissues, molecules, and atoms, and there is also activity in the subconscious and activity manifested in the intellect through emotions and thoughts. These faculties are integrated in the word yolli, life, the quality of knowing how to live precisely in constant activity. We already know from the word of the elders that wherever there is life there is movement.

Ollin yoliztli means precisely that, life and movement. With these two words a paired expression is formed in which the sense of duality is again perceived.

Ollin, autonomous activity in most of our organs, is related to the driving organ par excellence. Let us see: yolotl or yolohtli is the heart. From it arise the pulsations that activate the individual atlachinolli, burned water, whose name is yeztli, the hot liquid that runs through our arteries in rhythmic activity, impelling us to carry out actions with will and determination.

Ollin or ullin is also the name of a vegetable gum used as paint in ornamentation. It is the principal material for making the balls used in ulamaztli, the noun by which the pre-Hispanic ballgame is known. Ulli is the same gum that we now know as rubber or caoutchouc. Adding the possessive e at the end forms the word ulle, the gummy, pulsating, active thing. Rubber gum always vibrates with the slightest movement.

#### Tecpatl

Tecpatl translates as flint knife. It was an exceedingly important cutting instrument in ancient cultures. With the arrival of metal knives it fell into disuse. Nevertheless, we gathered some interesting opinions about these implements. Today, in several museums there are specimens of these large blades manufactured from various kinds of hard stone, the most common being those of dark obsidian.

> They say it means flint blade. We did not know them because they are no longer used. If they were of flint or some other very hard stone, sparks can be struck from them by hitting one against another to make fire. Bringing it close to tinder, a dry moss, it quickly catches fire. Just as the sparks catch, ideas come to us that we were not even expecting. As a knife, it serves us to cut little by little until we know what is inside things.

In ancient times there were stone knives for both domestic and industrial use. There were others in the form of extremely fine, thin, long blades used by the Mexica surgeons called texoxotlaticitl.

In matters of symbolism, and delving into the Indigenous word, tecpatl represents light, whether natural light or artificial light, such as that of a torch. As the product of two flints striking together, it represents ideas that spring like sparks from the depths of the human mind.

In this interpretation the extremely sharp tecpatl is the methodical analysis that sometimes arises from simple curiosity. With the knife, incisions are made in everything we wish to know, from the outside inward, opening layer after layer until reaching the depths of its structure to discover its original essence. In the Tezcatlipoca context, Itzpapalotl, the butterfly with flint knives, represents thoughts that cannot be retained. They fly swiftly from one place to another. By contrast, the ideas represented by tecpatl are those that originate through analysis and reflection, leading to profound knowledge that goes beyond the superficial.

Tecpatl also represents the season of intense cold. It is said that ehecatl, the wind, arms itself with extremely fine, curved obsidian knives that hurt and cut our skin.

*Tecpatl. Stone of the Suns.*

Applying these characteristics to people involved with the tecpatl sign, it can be determined that this sign represents the mental sharpness that awakens curiosity

#### Quiahuitl

Quiahuitl is the natural manifestation of the element rain. As an abstract element it is tlaloctli or Tlaloc, the liquid the earth drinks. It is the rain that waters the fields, awakening the seeds deposited in them and making new plants sprout, filling Mother Earth with greenery. This is what the testimonies gathered tell us:

> Quiahuitl is rain. Sometimes it is the breeze that refreshes us, and when it grows angry it is sometimes a storm or a heavy hailstorm that destroys everything. Water after all, it waters our fields so that the crops grow and we have good harvests. If it did not rain, we would have no ears of maize, beans, tomatoes, or squash. If it did not rain, the rivers would dry up and we would die of thirst. That is why we do not like drought.

*Tlaloc representing the Quiahuitl day. Plate 19, Borgia Codex.*

Associating the preceding opinions with what we know about the general behavior of the element rain produces these characteristics: there are early rains that descend in the form of frost during winter, and late rains that, in the autumn heat, cool the intensely hot atmosphere a little.

Rains sometimes arrive in the form of a damp, light breeze brought by the wind, although sometimes they come with the fury of a hurricane, bursting violently from the clouds and falling upon the earth in abundant streams.

In the traditions of some ancient peoples it is said that the tlaloqueh, from above the clouds, strike enormous clay vessels which, when broken, let the rains escape. The pieces of the vessels, colliding with one another, produce the electrical discharges that light up the sky during storms.

Quiahuitl is represented as a face in profile with an eye surrounded by an enormous circle. When two rain signs are brought together facing one another, the result is the face of Tlaloc.

#### Xochitl

The xochitl sign is the last of the twenty day signs. It is at the end of the group, closing the cycle of each twenty-day period. The opinions gathered were simple but very eloquent regarding the meaning of the flower in the application of the tonalamatl.

> Xochitl is flower. Flowers are the most beautiful things we find in life. The countryside is more beautiful when it fills with flowers, because there are colors and fragrances that gladden the heart. Just seeing them makes one very happy and gladdens the heart, comparing them with beautiful women. We also compare songs and verses with flowers. When trees fill with flowers, it is a sign that there will be fruit. In life everything must flower. Even we flower by working, doing good things for the family and for the village. When we finish each job we do, it is as though we gave a flower.

*Xochitl. Plate 33, Bodley Codex.*

Xochitl is everything beautiful and of quality. The message contained in the flower sign invites human beings to seek flowering thoughts and expressions, trying to direct all their activities toward full flowering in every aspect of life. This sign is also everything beautiful and flowering that the human mind can generate. People born under the flower sign are very creative and subtle in attaining an aim, which they achieve without haste and with the firm conviction that they will fulfill their commitments satisfactorily.

#### The importance of the meaning of numbers

We reiterate once again at the beginning of this chapter that the numbers accompanying the twenty signs and the four year bearers are thirteen. We sensed that each must have its own characteristics, so that each calendrical combination of sign and number would give an individual reading. The combination of the two factors, 20 signs and 13 numbers, would give us 260 different results.

For those of us who took part in this work in the 1980s, between 1983 and 1989, an incidental comment made years earlier, around 1974, by Dr. Paul Kirchhoff to Dr. Sergio Carmona Ríos, who was interested in pre-Hispanic medicine, was encouraging. It referred to paragraphs numbered 124 and 223 of the work Xiutlapoualli, belonging to Tezcacoacatl Cotzatzin, son of Apanecateuctli Xiuhcozcatl, published in 1976 as Toltec-Chichimec History. In the preface to that edition it says:

> ...Regarding calendars, we wish to mention that, as occurs in many sources, numerals associated with day names appear in personal names, and thus we have 2-Wind, 1-Serpent, 2-Death, and so forth. But in our source it is striking that in paragraph 223 there is a Chichimec person called Ce Yaotl, 1-War, and in paragraph 124 a Toltec-Chichimec called Chicnauc homitl, 9-Bone, is mentioned. This may imply that war and bone are day names we did not know. Perhaps the name bone is synonymous with miquiztli, death, and perhaps 1-War is the same as 1-Flint, since this is the calendrical name of Uitzilopochtli and Camaxtle, gods of war.

Apart from the concern that sources might contain day names other than the usual ones, the comment to Dr. Carmona concerned the special importance each number must have.

He said that in the Tecamachalco area in the state of Puebla he had heard it mentioned that in ancient times the numbers had different characteristics.

If a number had a value and a characteristic applicable to the accompanying day sign, it must also be applicable to each sign of the four year bearers.

#### Ome

Ome is number two.

Number two in Maya is ka.

Quetzalhuitzitzilli, companion of number two.

In nature it is the simplest manifestation of duality, which is found in everything symmetrical and even in everything that, although asymmetrical, has two complementary parts. Symmetry in nature amazes us with its perfection when we contemplate a butterfly's wings, the leaves of plants, or the feathers of precious birds.

The human body is not exempt from this symmetry, since most of its principal organs are dual, such as the lungs, the cerebral hemispheres, the testicles in men, and the ovaries in women. The arms with their hands and the legs with their feet have the same condition of symmetry and duality. Other organs that are single, such as the heart, also have duality in their principal parts. By generating duality we understand the essence that forms, according to traditional teachings, everything our eyes contemplate. That is literally what they tell us in general terms, before explaining that everything our eyes see, the stones, clouds, people, birds, trees, and plants, is made up of male parts and female parts. This simple explanation has also helped us better understand the comments of Fray Pedro de los Ríos, in which he mentions that in Omeyohcan, the place of dual essence, dwelt the Lord and Lady of Duality called Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl. On joining through the attractive force of Omeyotl, they form Ometeotl. Fray Pedro de los Ríos calls him Hometeule, attributing to him the qualities of the Christians' God the Father. In Indigenous thought, Omeyohcan is the place containing the essence of duality, Omeyotl, which is the force of attraction that joins particles and beings of opposite polarities, forming units that are plural in their integral essence.

Xochiquetzalli butterfly.

Ome as a noun derives from omitl, bones. With them, the shape of the human body and of all vertebrate animals is structured.

The symbolism of number two when applied to people is union, complement, formation, and ambivalence. To this we may add structure and inner solidity, because great value is placed on firm Indigenous values grounded in respect and in the principle or esteem of duality.

#### Nahui

Nahui, number four. In the Maya language it is kan, serpent, vibrating energy.

Four is one of the principal factors of calendrical mathematics, involving most of nature's rhythms. From the Indigenous point of view, people nourish themselves with the tonalli they receive from the four directions of the universe, giving life-giving strength to their body and spirit. Physically, number four contains the explanation of the natural sequence in the formation of the human being.

Zolin, the quail.

The origin of its name in the Mexican language may derive from the verb nacahuia, which means to cover with flesh or to embody.

With this number, a person's primary formation is completed, with everything received from nature from each of the directions and with the basic parts that compose the person as a collection of elements, always acting in functions in harmony with each other. Let us remember that number one, ce, is the whole representing the human being, and from two to four there are both the supporting structure, omitl, and the liquid current that animates it, eztli, and in four, nacahuic, nahui, that which envelops and gives shape to each person. This way of perceiving human beings may seem somewhat naive, but in numerical language the formative situations of the human body and spirit become clear through the positional order of the pre-Hispanic mathematical system as an evolutionary record of each person's natural development and a commitment to growth on several levels.

Muscles covering the human skeleton.

Number four is the coming together of the four basic elements: heat, wind, and water upon the generating earth. #### Macuilli

Macuilli is number five.

In the Maya language it is ho.

In ancient mathematical writing it is represented by a bar formed from five dots. In hieroglyphic writing it is represented by the drawing of a hand. At a particular moment in the lunar phases, thought becomes active, influencing intellect and intuition. Tlaloc represents the force of the hurricane and the storm, although he also manifests through gentle, caressing rains. He is the Lord of Clouds and Lightning, of unbreakable tenacity, and of the regenerating fertility of water.

The bird accompanying the number eight is the shining eagle, *itzcuauhtli* in the *Borgia Codex*, or simply *cuauhtli* in the *Borbónico Codex* and the *Tonalamatl de Aubin*, as a symbol of freedom and the high aspirations achieved with decisiveness and impulsiveness.

In the *Tonalamatl de Aubin*, Tlaloc's face emerges from the eagle's beak, reinforcing the influence of the Lord of Rain for people who carry his characteristics.

The summary is made up as follows:

**Chicueyi.** Integration through constant learning, gathering the experience of life from what surrounds us.

**Tlaloc.** Strength in decisions and gentleness in dealings. These are people with a changeable character that one must know how to keep at the center between extremes.

**Cuauhtli.** Splendor, decisiveness, impulsiveness, and high objectives. The eagle always seeks freedom of action, makes quick decisions, and hits the target precisely.

**Tlaloc.** Once again the Lord of Rain appears, reinforcing the qualities of the daytime companion.

#### Chicnahui

Chicnahui, the number nine, is *chicnahui* in the Nahuatl language. In the Maya language it is *bolon*.

It is represented by one bar and four dots. Its hieroglyph is a hand and four dots or four fingers.

It is closely related to wind and rain as providers of the conditions for life to flower. One of the calendrical names of some people within the specializations of the science of the Quetzalcoatl is Chicnahui ehecatl, 9 Wind.

In numerical symbolism, *chicnahui*, the number nine, is similar to five, *macuilli*, because it is centered in the two evolutionary processes forming the structure of the numbers, where, as they accumulate one by one, it is placed at the center of the number eight. In the count of destiny, Ehecatl Quetzalcoatl is the ninth of the Lords of the Days, and Tlaloc Quetzalcoatl is the ninth of the Lords of the Night. Therefore, nine is the second number of centered beings.

The companion of this number is Quetzalcoatl, the Beautiful Serpent of Life personified in several functions related to rain and wind, the Sun and the Earth.

The accompanying bird is the turkey, *huexolotl* in the Mexican language. This bird represents groundless strutting inflated by personal importance. The result of constant work to achieve a spirit of service is harmonious coexistence, which turns the *huexolotl* into a little jade turkey, *chalchiuhtotolin*. To become a Quetzalcoatl in any of the specialties required integrity, simplicity, and transparency.

Tlaloc's face emerges from the *huexolotl*'s beak, combining the characteristics of the strength and impetus of the Lord of Rain with the wisdom and transcendence of the Quetzalcoatl through the elevation of the spirit, even in the simplest and most everyday attitudes. An example of this combination in historical antiquity was the *Quezalcoatl tlaloc tlamacazque*, who today are the *teciuhtlazque* of our mountains, expert knowers of the rain element.

#### Matlactli

Matlactli is the number ten.

In Maya it is called *lahun*.

There are two bars with the value of ten dots. In the Nahuatl language, *matlactli* means bringing the hands together. The hieroglyph is formed by a diamond representing the joined hands, and symbolizes reaching half the realization of the twenty signs forming the count of destiny. In *matlactli* is the duality of our hands, for the skill of one joins the balance provided by the other.

The metaphor of hands coming together refers to work in dual integration, where it is said that *without both hands one cannot make tortillas*. It is also stated that *if one hand learns what the other can do, things are done with wisdom and skill, because they are made with the heart as well as with the hands*.

One hand is metaphorically the reflection of the other, like an image seen in a mirror. At the same time they complement one another. A mirror is the simplest way to begin to know ourselves as we really are. Perhaps for this reason the ancient Toltec wise people or their ancestors detected that the companion of the number ten is Tezcatlipoca.

Tezcatlipoca is one of the most important concepts of antiquity because it contains everything relating to memory, introversion, and inner knowledge. It is sensitivity, intuition, and the awakening of consciousness.

The bird accompanying the number ten is the *tecolotl*, who is the living representation of meditation, reflection, concentration, and therefore wisdom. As a complement of Tezcatlipoca, those related to the *tecolotl* make use of the forces activated at night and in darkness.

Beside the *tecolotl* emerges the face of Tezcatlipoca as well, reinforcing the qualities of the owl and of the barn owl that sometimes replaces the *tecolotl* as companion of the number ten.

#### Matlactli uan ce

Matlactli uan ce is the number eleven.

In Maya it is called *buluk*. It is represented with two bars and one dot. Matlactli uan ce literally is ten and one. In evolutionary symbolism it is a rest, a pause in the process of transformation, to continue afterward along the ascending path of the spiral. It is grace, lightness and superficiality, ease of expression, and in nature, splendor, color, and pleasure inviting leisure and amusement as part of a break necessary to complete the third stage of accumulated experience, arriving at centrality once more through what has been achieved, represented at the end of the series in the number thirteen.

*Xochipilli; alotl and Chalmecatecuhtli.*

Accompanying the number eleven we have the Lord of Rest and Stillness, granting precisely rest to the qualities of the sign corresponding to the numeral in the sequence. Mictlantecuhtli is present as Chalmecatecuhtli, the Lord of Those of Chalman. A Chalmecatecuhtli, and in the feminine case Chalmecacihuatl, the Lady of Chalman, according to the description of Nahuatl speakers of the northern Sierra of Puebla, are people who have known or experienced the moment of death and acquired the power to see in obsidian mirrors. In an ordinary person this can be translated as the quality of constant reflection before deciding to act in any circumstance.

The bird accompanying Chalmecatecuhtli is a macaw, called *alotl* or *chiconcuetzalin* in the Mexican language. With colorful plumage, it spends its time doing nothing really important, very introverted about its own affairs, although it likes to chatter, since it has great ease with words.

From the macaw's beak emerges the face of Xochipilli, the Child of Flowers, balancing, through the relationship with botany and love of nature, the pause of the number eleven toward the flow of the sign with which it is combined.

**Matlactli uan ce.** Rest, a pause in the process of evolution, to continue afterward with greater impetus.

**Chalmecatecuhtli.** A quality centered on the power of reflection.

**Alotl.** Lightness in attitudes, color in dress, but very introverted.

**Xochipilli.** Creativity manifested through the relationship with botany.

Matlactli uan ome is ten and two. In the Maya language it is *lahak*.

It is represented with two bars and two dots. In hieroglyphic writing there are two hands and two dots.

#### Matlactli huan yei

Matlactli uan yei is ten and three.

In Maya it is called *oxlahun*.

It is represented by two bars and three dots.

The number thirteen is one of the most important factors in pre-Cuauhtémoc mathematics, as a fundamental figure for knowing both the cycles of the moving celestial bodies in the Celestial Bowl and the gestation cycles of many beings that live upon the earth. In the case of human beings, to form within the vessel of the female womb, they take thirteen times twenty days and once thirteen, from the moment the precious seed is deposited until the instant the new being draws in the *ihiyotl*, the breath of life, upon leaving the maternal womb.

*Xochiquetzal with the Toznene and Citlalinicue. Tonalamatl de Aubin.*

Thirteen times twenty is two hundred and sixty days, the complete count of a Tonalpohualli, with the combination of the probabilities of destiny offering us several paths and the responsibility to choose the right one to develop the potential nature grants us.

In human beings, the number thirteen manifests in interweaving their lives, their undertakings, and their achieved objectives with those of all living beings, within a framework of respect for everything that exists.

The number thirteen is flowering.

The metaphor tells us that one who possesses the essence of the number thirteen *is one who, having flowered, is in a position to bear fruit*.

The number thirteen is accompanied in the *Borbónico Codex* by Citlalin i cue, she who has her skirt of stars, as a feminine energy released from stellar groups, flooding the Earth with that essence of life. Eduard Seler identifies Citlalin i cue as Ilamatecuhtli, a representation of the Earth in her stage of maturity. In other sources only Cihuacoatl is mentioned, a woman with the body of a serpent who represents the energy accumulated in the Earth's skirt.

*Xiochiquetzalli.*

Let us recall what we have already set out on previous pages. In ancient times, by recording the elements of nature in human form, they left us a legacy of information in the *amoxtin* and in the pre-Hispanic sculptures of different periods and peoples. A person linked by the calendrical system with Tlazolteotl acts by transforming their surroundings positively, generating ideas and helping loved ones in a constant resurgence, characteristics similar to those of Mictlancihuatl, a representation of the change that effectively takes place in Tlazolteotl's skirt.

#### The essence of life from a cosmic principle and from the four directions of the world

Everything is dual energy; it manifests in our thoughts, ideas, attitudes, and emotions in the psychic sphere, and is physically implicit in heat, sound, light, colors, electricity, and the magnetism given off by bodies under certain conditions. We are surrounded by emanations of various energies generated by the elements of nature, by everything that has life, and by what is apparently inanimate. From the four directions of the universe and the four corners of the world, these emanations of dual essence nourish our tonalli; we are penetrated by them. We generate and emit dual energy in our movements, in our words, and even in our intentions. We relate to everything that exists in our environment through vibrations or emanations of that dual nature; therefore, we are psychic essence in Tezcatlipoca and we are essence of life with the Quetzalcoatl that is implicit within us from the beginning of our formation and fully manifests from the moment of our birth.

Neither Sahagún, Diego Durán, nor any other of the early religious chroniclers ever imagined that these calculations form Ilhuicamatiliztli, knowledge of the sky. Ilhuicamatiliztli or ilhuicaxicalmatiliztli are words you will never find written in the sources, but which are common among Indigenous Nahuas.

From the viewpoint of the observers of the sky in antiquity, the great celestial vault resembles an enormous inverted bowl filled with points of light.

Undoubtedly, all their observations were made from Earth; therefore, it is truly the center from which the movements of all the celestial bodies visible in the sky are perceived.

#### Knowledge of the sky (Ilhuicamatiliztli)

> What is it, a blue-green bowl filled with the popcorn they call momochitl?

This saying from Book VI of the Florentine Codex refers to the sky we see strewn with stars.

The mathematical combination of the cycles of Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli, Venus, with the cycles of Tonatiuh, the Sun, and Metztli, the Moon, forms a very well-known symbol called a quintiduo or quincunx. It is a glyph generally formed by a square and five points which, to give it a name, we will call Ilhuicamacuilli, the number five of the sky, so that the reader may continue to become familiar with the Indigenous language and thought. The great bowl of the sky is filled with countless points of light of different sizes and appearances. Most resemble little eyes that continually blink, holding a fixed position in relation to other stars, forming figures in the sky as a group, such as Citlalcolohtli, which has the shape of a scorpion, or Mamalhuaztli, which resembles the pieces of wood or spinning sticks with which the New Fire was kindled in years with the number and sign Ome Acatl, Two Reed, every fifty-two years, beginning the Earth's bioenergetic cycles governed by Omacatl Tezcatlipoca.

Mamalhuaztli.

There are other points of light that move very rapidly, leaving behind a trail like floating hair. They take a long time to be seen again by human beings, and it is said that few of them exist. They are known as Tzoncitlaltin because of their tail of light resembling Xochiquetzalli's hair.

There are other luminous points that break away from the sky toward Earth, as though deliberately letting themselves fall, quickly fading in the blackness of night. These are called Citlalhuetzi, and it is common to hear their song in the whistle of their fall in the night sky.

In the Celestial Bowl, a great band is clearly defined, beginning in Tlahuiztlanpa, the place of light, crossing the sky to Cihuatlanpa, the place of women, dividing the sky into two halves. This celestial band is Ilhuicaohtli, the path followed by the heavenly bodies of steady light, those that do not blink, a band that also cuts in two the Cloud Serpent called Mixcoatl, apparently made from the dust of the stars. Citlalohtli is another name for Ilhuicaohtli, constantly observed by human beings, for within it move the heavenly bodies that guide them in governing their daily lives. Another name by which it is known in the Sierra of Hidalgo is Citlaltlachtli, the playing field of the heavenly bodies, in which their movements are bounded by the Ecliptic.

By moving in recurring cycles and constant rhythms, they served the ancestors of many generations in creating astronomical records in their time tables.

The Sun, Tonatiuh, and Metztli, the Moon, move along this path, each with its own cycles and its own sequence.

One of the heavenly bodies moving along this path has such brilliant luminosity that its nearness or distance can be appreciated with the naked eye through the intensity of the light, as though it were a mirror reflecting the light of the Sun. When its presence in the sky is as the morning star before the Sun rises, its name is Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli, the Lord of Dawn, which is when it advances ahead of the Sun. When it moves after the Sun, we see it in the afternoons and at nightfall; then it is called Xolotl, the twin or companion.

As the principal elements of the count of time called Cauhpohualli or Tlaxiuhpohualli, the cycles of Tonatiuh, Metztli, and Xolotl-Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli were chiefly recorded.

The cosmic instant at which the periods of these three heavenly bodies join served those who keep the Counts of Time, the Tonalpouhque of the most remote antiquity, to begin counting the cycles, taking as their measure the rhythm of the Sun in the Tlapohualxihuitl with respect to Earth. From Earth, Tonatiuh is observed occupying the same place in the sky after an average of three hundred sixty-five and one-quarter days has passed. > Tonatiuh: each time it again occupies the same place in space is taken as one complete solar period.

> Xolotl-Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli: each time it completes five cycles as seen from Earth, exactly eight years, chicueyi xihuitl, have passed on Earth.

All these records are condensed in the glyph inscribed on numerous astronomical monuments throughout the territory of Anahuac, with some variations but without losing the original design.

The mathematical relationship of the five-point symbol to a period of eight years is unquestionable:

Every eight years, five times twenty periods of Metztli pass, counted from its birth until it again disappears from our sight. The average calculation contained in Ilhuicamacuilli is two thousand nine hundred twenty-two elapsed days.

Every eight years, Xolotl-Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli completes five of its cycles.

Every eight years, Tonatiuh completes eight of its cycles.

From these three principal records it is deduced that Ilhuicamacuilli represents eight elapsed years and shows us beyond doubt that the mathematical relationship of these heavenly bodies' cycles is the foundation for the count of time.

#### Mathematical verification

The average cycle for one complete lunation, based on the calculation of 1300 lunations occurring in 104 years, is set at 29 days, 5 hours, 16 minutes, and 48 seconds, which, expressed in the decimal system, averages 29.22 days.

The time taken by the Morning and Evening Star to return to the same place in the sky is 584 days, 9 hours, and 36 minutes; this period converted to decimals is 584.40 days.

For the Sun we have an average of 365 days and 6 hours, or 365.25 days in decimal form. The actual time is 365 days, 5 hours, 49 minutes, and 46 seconds.
