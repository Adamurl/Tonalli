# Cozcacuauhtli — Vulture

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 16 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Vulture; often rendered “king vulture” in iconographic studies
- **Glyph description:** Vulture head with distinctive fleshy or colored features.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Itzpapalotl

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Cozcacuauhtli* refers to a **vulture**, frequently identified iconographically with a king-vulture-like bird.

## Core meanings and symbolism

As a scavenger, the vulture is ecologically linked with carrion; renewal through consumption is an interpretive observation, not a fixed ancient gloss. Sahagún connects the 1-Vulture trecena with elders and long life while noting that not all share that fate [S1].

## Cultural and historical context

Vultures transform carrion into life for themselves and occupy a boundary between death, decay, sky, and renewal. That ecological reality made the bird visually distinctive, although we should not assume one universal symbolic definition.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Itzpapalotl** as associated with the Cozcacuauhtli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Cozcacuauhtli means “Vulture.” Sahagún describes the 1-Vulture trecena as associated with elders, prosperity, and long life, while explicitly noting that not every person born within it would share the same destiny. The passage is a useful reminder that Nahua divination was not mechanically deterministic.**
