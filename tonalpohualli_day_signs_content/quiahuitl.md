# Quiahuitl — Rain

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 19 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Rain
- **Glyph description:** Falling rain, rain drops, or a rain-associated face.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Tonatiuh

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Quiahuitl* means **rain**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Rain was essential to crops and agricultural timing. It belongs to a sacred geography of mountains, clouds, caves, and springs [S2, S3]. Rain nourishes but storms, hail, and floods can be destructive. Broad rain symbolism should not be conflated with a specific manuscript’s patron assignment.

## Cultural and historical context

Rain was one of the most consequential forces in central Mexican life. Agricultural success depended on the timing and amount of rainfall, and the rain-and-mountain complex centered on Tlaloc, the Tlaloque, sacred mountains, caves, springs, and agricultural fertility.

Rain could be sustaining but also destructive—hail, flood, and storm belonged to the same powerful atmospheric world.

A day-sign patron table and the broad religious meaning of rain are not identical. Although Tlaloc is the best-known rain deity, the patron listed for the individual Quiahuitl sign in a particular calendrical scheme may be another deity. The app should cite the specific manuscript tradition used for patron fields.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Tonatiuh** as associated with the Quiahuitl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Quiahuitl means “Rain.” Rain sustained maize agriculture and tied human communities to mountains, clouds, springs, and powerful rain deities. Its sacred importance came from that double capacity to nourish life and to arrive with dangerous force.**
