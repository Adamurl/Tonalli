# Acatl — Reed

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 13 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Reed
- **Glyph description:** Reed stalks or a bound reed.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Tezcatlipoca

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Acatl* means **reed**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Reeds belonged to central Mexico’s wetland ecology and supplied materials for construction, mats, and other technologies. Acatl is one of four year-bearers [S2, S6]. Ce Acatl appears in traditions concerning Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl, though later narratives vary and require care.

## Cultural and historical context

Reeds were common plants in the lacustrine environment of central Mexico and were used in construction, mats, tools, and other everyday technologies. “Reed” also occurs in major calendrical names and mytho-historical dates.

The year **Ce Acatl (1 Reed)** is famous because colonial traditions link it with Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl, though later historical narratives about these dates must be handled cautiously.

Acatl is one of the **four year bearers**.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Tezcatlipoca** as associated with the Acatl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.
