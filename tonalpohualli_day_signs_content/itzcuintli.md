# Itzcuintli — Dog

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 10 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Dog
- **Glyph description:** Dog head.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Mictlantecuhtli

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Itzcuintli* means **dog**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Dogs were familiar household animals and also had mortuary associations. Central Mexican sources describe dogs helping the dead journey toward Mictlan [S1, S2], connecting the sign with passage between the living world and the realm of the dead without making the animal inherently negative.

## Cultural and historical context

Dogs had practical roles in Mesoamerican households, but they also carried strong mortuary associations. Colonial and archaeological sources describe dogs as beings capable of assisting the dead in the difficult journey through the underworld, especially in relation to crossing water on the path toward Mictlan.

This helps explain why dog symbolism can sit naturally beside the death-and-underworld complex without making the animal itself “negative.”

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Mictlantecuhtli** as associated with the Itzcuintli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.
