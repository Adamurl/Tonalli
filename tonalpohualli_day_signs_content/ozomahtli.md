# Ozomahtli — Monkey

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 11 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Monkey
- **Glyph description:** Monkey head, typically understood as a spider monkey.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Xochipili

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Ozomahtli* means **monkey**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Monkey imagery is associated with music, dance, and performance [S1, S2]. The Xochipilli association places it near artistic activity, though patron mappings vary by tradition. Sahagún’s 1-Monkey account includes singing and sociability, and also connects the trecena with the Cihuateteo. Joyful and dangerous associations coexist; this is not a universal personality profile.

## Cultural and historical context

Monkey imagery became closely associated with **music, dance, performance, pleasure, and artistic activity**. This connection appears in several central Mexican sources and fits the broader religious sphere of Xochipilli/Macuilxochitl.

Sahagún’s discussion of **Ce Ozomahtli (1 Monkey)** describes people born under that trecena in terms that include sociability and singing. It also records a more ominous dimension: the trecena was associated with the descent of the **Cihuateteo**, dangerous spirits connected with women who died in childbirth, and families protected young children from them.

That combination is important: a single trecena could carry both celebratory and dangerous sacred qualities.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Xochipili** as associated with the Ozomahtli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.
