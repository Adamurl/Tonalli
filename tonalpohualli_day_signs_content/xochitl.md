# Xochitl — Flower

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 20 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Flower
- **Glyph description:** A stylized flower.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Xochiquetzal

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Xochitl* means **flower** and closes the sequence of twenty day signs.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Flowers evoke beauty, fragrance, and preciousness, and can carry fertility, sexuality, offering, and ritual associations [S2, S7]. “Flower and song” (*in xochitl in cuicatl*) evokes elevated artistic expression. Durán connects the 1-Flower trecena with artisans; that account is not a universal reading of every Xochitl day [S7].

## Cultural and historical context

Flowers were exceptionally rich signs in Nahua culture. They belonged to **beauty, fragrance, fertility, sexuality, offerings, song, poetry, preciousness, and artistic creation**. The paired expression *in xochitl in cuicatl*—“flower and song”—became a way of speaking about elevated artistic, poetic, and philosophical expression in Nahua traditions.

Flower imagery is closely connected with deities such as **Xochiquetzal** and Xochipilli.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Xochiquetzal** as associated with the Xochitl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Xochitl means “Flower.” Flowers were symbols of beauty, fertility, fragrance, offerings, artistry, song, and precious expression. In colonial descriptions of the divinatory calendar, the 1-Flower trecena is especially connected with artistic and skilled work.**
