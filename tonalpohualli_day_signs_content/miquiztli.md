# Miquiztli — Death

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 6 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Death
- **Glyph description:** A skull or skeletal death-head.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Tecciztecatl

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Miquiztli* means **death**. Its glyph usually uses unmistakable skeletal imagery.

## Core meanings and symbolism

The skull glyph names death directly. Nahua sources describe differentiated postmortem destinations; in broader religious contexts, death, sacrifice, decomposition, and fertility could be intertwined. Sahagún describes 1-Miquiztli as mixed, while Durán gives a darker account [S1, S7], cautioning against a universal “bad luck” gloss.

## Cultural and historical context

In Nahua thought, death was not simply the abstract opposite of life. Different forms of death were tied to different postmortem destinations and ritual meanings. Death imagery belonged to a larger cosmic system in which decomposition, sacrifice, fertility, and renewal could be closely connected.

The historical sources warn us against assuming that a sign named “Death” was automatically evil. Sahagún describes **Ce Miquiztli (1 Death)** as mixed—having favorable and unfavorable positions—and says devotion and ritual conduct mattered. Durán gives a darker assessment. The disagreement itself is evidence that the tradition should not be flattened into one modern keyword.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Tecciztecatl** as associated with the Miquiztli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Miquiztli means “Death” and is represented by a skull. Although its name can sound ominous to a modern reader, sixteenth-century sources do not give it one simple meaning: Sahagún and Durán preserve different assessments, and the fortune of a date depended on its full calendrical setting.**
