# Mazatl — Deer

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 7 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Deer
- **Glyph description:** Deer head, usually recognizable by ears and antlers.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Tlaloc

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Mazatl* means **deer**. Deer were economically and ritually important animals across Mesoamerica, associated with hunting, wilderness, meat, hides, bone, and offerings.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Deer evoke the landscape beyond cultivation and relationships involving hunting and subsistence. They provided meat and useful materials and also appear in ritual and visual traditions [S2, S3]. The sign brings this consequential animal into sacred time.

## Cultural and historical context

The deer belongs strongly to **the hunted landscape beyond cultivated settlement**. In Mesoamerican imagery more broadly, deer appear in hunting and sacrificial contexts and as calendrical beings.

The day sign therefore draws on a real ecological and ritual relationship, not merely an abstract emblem.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Tlaloc** as associated with the Mazatl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.
