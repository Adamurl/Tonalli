# Malinalli — Grass / Twisted Grass

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 12 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Grass; often “twisted grass”
- **Glyph description:** A twisted bundle or tuft of grass.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Patecatl

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Malinalli* refers to **grass**, often represented in a twisted or bundled form. The visual form matters because grasses and fibers were useful materials woven, twisted, tied, and transformed.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Malinalli evokes grass in cultivated and uncultivated landscapes. Plant fibers could be twisted, tied, woven, and made useful; the bundled glyph may suggest prepared material. Some patron tables connect it with Patecatl and medicinal/pulque associations [S2, S3]. “Transformation” is an interpretation, not the lexical meaning.

## Cultural and historical context

Grass belongs to the cultivated and uncultivated landscape and to everyday technologies such as rope, mats, bindings, and woven material. Boone notes that the day-sign vocabulary includes plants and natural substances that would have been immediately recognizable in ordinary life.

The common patron association with **Patecatl** also links Malinalli in some calendrical tables with the medicinal and pulque complex.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Patecatl** as associated with the Malinalli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Malinalli is usually translated as “Grass” and is often shown as a twisted or bundled plant. Its image draws from the ordinary plant materials that surrounded Nahua life and could be cut, woven, bound, and transformed into useful objects.**

## Editorial cautions

Avoid claiming “Malinalli means transformation” as if it were the literal definition; that is an interpretation, not the lexical meaning.
