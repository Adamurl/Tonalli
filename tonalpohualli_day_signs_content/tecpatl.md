# Tecpatl — Flint

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 18 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Flint / Flint knife
- **Glyph description:** A chipped flint blade or personified flint knife.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Chalchihuihtotolin

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Técpatl* means **flint**, especially the sharp stone blade.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Flint was shaped into useful blades and tools; flint knives also had ritual importance and could be personified [S2]. Their sacrificial use does not make every Tecpatl date a prediction of violence. Tecpatl is a year-bearer [S6], and Sahagún’s 1-Flint account describes trecena-specific ritual attention to Huitzilopochtli and Camaxtli [S1].

## Cultural and historical context

Flint blades were cutting tools but also carried profound ritual importance because sacrificial knives could be represented as animate, divine objects. In central Mexican imagery, flint could be given a face and teeth, emphasizing that ritual implements possessed sacred agency rather than being merely inert tools.

Tecpatl is one of the **four year-bearer signs**.

The trecena **Ce Tecpatl (1 Flint)** was associated in Sahagún’s account with major ritual attention to Huitzilopochtli and Camaxtli, illustrating how a calendrical sign could organize ceremonial action.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Chalchihuihtotolin** as associated with the Tecpatl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

> **Tecpatl means “Flint.” Its glyph is a sharpened stone blade, an object with both practical and powerful ritual uses. Flint knives could even be personified in sacred imagery. Tecpatl is also one of the four signs used to name years.**
