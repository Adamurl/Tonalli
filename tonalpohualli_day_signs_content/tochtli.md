# Tochtli — Rabbit

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 8 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Rabbit
- **Glyph description:** Rabbit head, typically with long ears.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Mayahuel

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Tochtli* means **rabbit**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Rabbit reproduction can evoke fertility and abundance, but this is an interpretive association, not a universal divinatory verdict. Tochtli is closely linked with the maguey/pulque complex and the Centzon Totochtin (“Four Hundred Rabbits”) [S1, S2, S7]. Pulque had ceremonial and social roles. “Cyclical excess” is a cautious synthesis of varied intoxication associations, not a literal translation. Tochtli is also a year-bearer [S2, S6].

## Cultural and historical context

Rabbit imagery has a rich relationship with the **maguey/pulque complex**. The divine beings known collectively as the **Centzon Totochtin (“Four Hundred Rabbits”)** personified the many forms or effects of pulque intoxication. The calendrical name **Ome Tochtli (2 Rabbit)** became especially important in this context.

That does not mean every Tochtli day means “drunkenness.” Rabbit imagery also belongs to wider themes of ritual feasting, fertility, maguey, and specific divine complexes.

Tochtli is also one of the **four year-bearer signs**.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Mayahuel** as associated with the Tochtli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description
