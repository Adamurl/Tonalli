# Atl — Water

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 9 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Water
- **Glyph description:** Flowing or curling water, often rendered as blue streams with shell or jewel-like elements.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Xiuhtecuhtli

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Atl* means **water**, one of the central substances of Nahua cosmology and daily survival.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Water sustains life and agriculture, linking the sign with rain, crops, lakes, and urban life in the Basin of Mexico. Springs, rivers, lakes, and rain belong to overlapping sacred and environmental relationships [S2, S3]. Water nourishes but can also flood and exceed human control.

## Cultural and historical context

Rain, springs, lakes, rivers, irrigation, agriculture, mountains, and fertility formed overlapping sacred relationships. Tlaloc and Chalchiuhtlicue are among the best-known water-associated deities, but water symbolism extends far beyond one god.

Tenochtitlan itself existed within an intensely managed lacustrine environment. Water was therefore simultaneously **life-giving, agricultural, urban, dangerous, and sacred**.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Xiuhtecuhtli** as associated with the Atl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Atl means “Water.” Its glyph represents the substance essential to rain, agriculture, lakes, springs, and life in the Basin of Mexico. Water could nourish and sustain, but it also belonged to powerful divine and environmental forces beyond human control.**
