# Ocelotl — Jaguar

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 14 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Jaguar / large spotted feline
- **Glyph description:** Jaguar or spotted feline head.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Tlazolteotl

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Ocelotl* denotes the **jaguar** or a powerful spotted feline.

## Core meanings and symbolism

The jaguar’s predatory strength informs its visual charge. Its nocturnal habits can evoke night, caves, and underworld settings; jaguar imagery also appears in royal and martial contexts and can carry sacred force [S2]. Sahagún’s unfavorable account concerns the 1-Ocelotl trecena, not every Ocelotl day [S1].

## Cultural and historical context

The jaguar was one of the most charged animals in Mesoamerican visual culture. Its nocturnal habits, strength, predatory power, and ability to move through dense landscape made it an important image of **night, earth, caves, rulership, warfare, and supernatural force** in different contexts.

The Mexica elite military identity commonly called the “jaguar warrior” shows one martial use of feline imagery, although the calendar sign should not be reduced to warfare.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Tlazolteotl** as associated with the Ocelotl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Ocelotl means “Jaguar.” The jaguar was a major symbol of predatory strength and nocturnal power throughout Mesoamerica and appears in royal, martial, underworld, and sacred imagery. In the tonalpohualli, however, its exact augury depended on the numbered day and trecena rather than on the feline image alone.**
