# Coatl — Serpent

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 5 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Serpent / Snake
- **Glyph description:** A serpent, often represented by the head and curved body.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Chalchihuitlicue

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Coatl* means **serpent**. Serpents appear throughout Mesoamerican religious imagery and could connect earth, water, fertility, sky, rulership, and supernatural power depending on context.

## Core meanings and symbolism

Serpents belong to a widespread sacred visual vocabulary [S2]. Depending on context they can connect earth, water, fertility, sky, rulership, or divine force. Quetzalcoatl’s feathered-serpent image joins serpent and precious-feather imagery, but does not define every serpent. In the Caso correlation, 1-Coatl is the anchor associated with August 13, 1521 Julian [S6].

## Cultural and historical context

It would be wrong to reduce every serpent image to one concept. **Quetzalcoatl (“Feathered Serpent”)** combines serpent and precious-feather imagery, while other serpentine beings belong to different environmental and divine complexes.

For the day sign, the safest approach is to retain the serpent as a powerful **living category of sacred imagery** rather than assign one personality trait.

Coatl is especially important for this app because the Alfonso Caso correlation commonly uses **1-Coatl** as the tonalpohualli date corresponding to 13 August 1521 (Julian), the fall of Tenochtitlan.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Chalchihuitlicue** as associated with the Coatl day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.

## App-safe description

> **Coatl means “Serpent.” Serpents were among the most important visual beings in Mesoamerican religion, appearing in imagery related to earth, water, fertility, celestial forces, and divine power. The day sign belongs to that broad sacred visual world rather than to one fixed character type.**
