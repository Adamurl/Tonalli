# Cuauhtli — Eagle

## Quick reference

- **Position:** 15 of 20
- **Literal translation:** Eagle
- **Glyph description:** Eagle head with a strong hooked beak.
- **Day-sign deity listed by AztecCalendar.com:** Xipe Totec

## Literal meaning and glyph

*Cuauhtli* means **eagle**.

## Core meanings and symbolism

The eagle’s flight evokes sky and height, while central Mexican traditions connect eagle imagery with solar force, warfare, sacrifice, and political authority [S2, S3]. The eagle is central to the Mexica foundation narrative of Tenochtitlan, one strand of the sign’s broader history.

## Cultural and historical context

The eagle was deeply tied to **the sun, height, warfare, sacrifice, and elite power**. The Mexica foundation narrative of Tenochtitlan famously centers on the eagle appearing on the cactus, and eagle imagery also formed part of elite military identity.

Yet the eagle was not only a political emblem. Its flight and relation to the sky made it a natural vehicle for solar and celestial symbolism.

## Day-sign deity association

AztecCalendar.com lists **Xipe Totec** as associated with the Cuauhtli day sign. This is a source-specific calendrical mapping, not a claim that every manuscript or scholar uses the same assignment. Keep it distinct from the deity associated with a **day numeral** and the patron of a **13-day trecena**; a full date can involve all these layers.
